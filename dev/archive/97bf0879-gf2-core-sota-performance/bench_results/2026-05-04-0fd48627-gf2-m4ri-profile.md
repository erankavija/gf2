The IPC drop from 5.62 → 3.73 (−34 %) when scaling from `n=1024` to `n=4096` is the most consequential observation. Frontend stalls remain pinned at zero, branch prediction is effectively perfect (< 0.4 % miss-rate), and DRAM bandwidth never exceeds ~1 % of peak. The bottleneck is therefore back-end and not main-memory.

## Bottleneck classification

**Primary bottleneck (post-PPC remaining gap to M4RI): (b) ILP / back-end execution-port throughput, becoming load-store-port-bound at the larger size.**

Justification, anchored to the perf-counter evidence:

1. **Not (a) memory-bandwidth / cache-miss bound.** Demand DRAM bandwidth at `n=4096` is ~403 MB/s — about 0.8 % of the host's DDR4-3200 ~50 GB/s peak (perf-stat.txt:235–238). The L2 demand hit rate is 99.3 % even at `n=4096` despite the working set (~6 MB across A, B, C plus a 32 KB Gray-code panel) overflowing the 512 KiB per-core L2 by ~12×; the register-tiled schedule (`use_register_tiled_schedule()` / `multiply_register_tiled()` per the example wiring at `m4rm_multiply_perfstat.rs`) confines the inner-loop access pattern to the active panel + tile, which is what keeps L2 hit-rate high. **Memory bandwidth is not the gap.**

2. **Not (c) algorithmic — at the M4RM-vs-M4RI level.** M4RI's adaptive-table-size gives it ~2.4× / ~3.3× headroom at `n=1024 / n=4096` over the current gf2-core M4RM ([`2026-04-29-strassen-matmul-crossover.md`](2026-04-29-strassen-matmul-crossover.md) § Comparison-to-pinned). That headroom is real and material — it justifies the existence of issues `380e041a` and `8e305c21`. But the perf-counter signature this report measures is *not* the algorithm's signature: at `n=1024` the gf2-core kernel runs at 5.62 IPC, near Zen 3's 4-wide retirement port budget once 64-bit-ALU XOR throughput limits and the DSB µop-cache hit rate are factored in (perf-stat.txt:83–88). A purely "wrong algorithm" gap would show up as wasted instructions, low IPC, or a stalled-frontend signature; we see the opposite at `n=1024`, and at `n=4096` the IPC drop is correlated with a 46 % rise in L1d miss-rate, not with a frontend stall (which holds at 0.02 %). The algorithmic dimension is real but is **not the dominant signal in this counter set**; it is also the question the downstream optimisation issues exist to test.

3. **Not (d) SIMD lane utilisation.** The kernel already dispatches to AVX2 SIMD via `gf2-kernels-simd` (the runtime PPC SIMD dispatcher; per `2026-04-27-asm-audit.md` all five `gf2-kernels-simd::x86` kernels emit YMM/256-bit code post-PPC). The IPC of 5.62 at `n=1024` is consistent with SIMD-XOR throughput close to the back-end ceiling — bumping lane width to 512-bit ZMM is not on the table on Zen 3 (no AVX-512 hardware on the 5900X host; see `CLAUDE.md` § Breakdown-time feasibility check); and AVX2 lane utilisation is already high.

4. **Not (e) thread-level parallelism gap.** The profiling binary is single-threaded by construction (perf-stat.txt:248) and the strassen-crossover criterion baseline is also single-threaded; the M4RI reference numbers in `2026-04-26-reference.csv` are likewise single-threaded. The remaining gap at the same threading level is therefore not a TLP question.

5. **Why (b) is the dominant signal.** The headline IPC drop 5.62 → 3.73 from `n=1024` → `n=4096` (−34 %) is correlated tightly with the L1d miss-rate rise 10.5 % → 15.3 % (+46 %); the L2-from-DC capture confirms the misses are not turning into LLC traffic (99.3 % L2 hit) and not turning into DRAM traffic (~0.8 % of peak). What changes is the *number of in-flight load/store ops* the back-end has to retire per Gray-table accumulator update. At `n=4096` the working set leaves L1d, every L1d miss costs a few cycles of back-end load-port queueing, and the kernel transitions from "compute-bound near retirement-port ceiling" to "load-store-port-bound on the Gray-table fanout." Frontend stalls remain pinned at zero and dTLB pressure remains negligible (perf-stat.txt:91–97; `n=4096` 1.09 M misses / 30 multiplies ≈ 36 K misses per multiply, well within Zen 3 L2 dTLB reach). This is the textbook signature of a back-end / load-store-port bound, *not* a memory-bandwidth bound.

The IPC drop and L1d miss-rate rise — not DRAM bandwidth, not frontend, not branch mis-prediction, not TLP — are what the downstream optimisation work has to attack.

## Implications for downstream optimisation issues

This is hand-off only. No design decisions are made here.

- **`380e041a` (prototype M4RI-style table scheduling).** The 2.4× / 3.3× ratio gap to pinned M4RI at `n=1024 / n=4096` (Measurements table) sits on top of a kernel that is at IPC 5.62 at the cache-resident size and back-end-bound at the LLC-streaming size. A useful prototype direction is to test whether an adaptive-`k` Gray-table schedule (i.e. M4RI's signature trick of choosing a per-block table width that fits L1d) can shrink the per-multiply L1d footprint at `n=4096` enough to keep the cache-resident IPC profile (5.62) instead of dropping to 3.73. The L2-from-DC capture (perf-stat.txt:223–238) suggests the headroom exists — DRAM bandwidth is not yet near the wall, so a wider Gray-table that *fits* at `n=4096` is a coherent question for `380e041a` to answer, even though the profile here does not commit to any specific `k` schedule.

- **`8e305c21` (production GF(2) matmul improvement).** Whatever wins in `380e041a` needs a production landing path. The post-PPC M4RM is at 0.42× / 0.31× of M4RI; the parent epic's 1.5× SOTA target (`97bf0879`) is not yet met. The bottleneck this profile measured — load-store-port-bound at the larger `n`, with ample DRAM headroom — is the cell `8e305c21` is implicitly being asked to attack. The profile does not commit to a kernel layout change, a panel-size schedule, or a Strassen-family alternative; it commits only to the bottleneck classification.

The downstream issues should treat the IPC 5.62 (`n=1024`, cache-resident) figure as the **headroom ceiling** they are trying to preserve as `n` grows, not the IPC 3.73 figure they are trying to beat in isolation. The ceiling matters because the same kernel achieves it at `n=1024` — the question is at what algorithm/schedule change the ceiling can be held at `n=4096`.

## § 0 TL;DR

- **GF(p) spmv** (4 cells, fflas-ffpack canonical): gf2-core lands at **0.23×–1.31×** of fflas-ffpack throughput — only the Mersenne-31 cell beats fflas at 1.31×; GF(7) (0.40×), GF(65521) (0.46×), and GF(251) (0.23×) are in-scope gaps below the 1.5× contract.
- **GF(p) sparse×dense** (4 cells, fflas-ffpack canonical): gf2-core is **0.05×–0.76×** of fflas-ffpack — Mersenne-31 reaches 0.76×; the small-prime cells (GF(7) 0.21×, GF(65521) 0.21×, GF(251) 0.05×) are large gaps reflecting fflas-ffpack's `int64_t / float / double` Modular dispatch over CSR-of-fp values, vs gf2-core's scalar Montgomery `Fp<P>` per-element multiply.
- **GF(2) spmv** (13 layout/structured/coding-theory variants, gf2-core self-canonical): CSC dual is fastest at 1.46 Gops/s; CSR 1.01 Gops/s; RCM-permuted-ER fastest at 1.30 Gops/s; the four coding-theory matrices (DVB-T2 short/normal, NR 5G BG1 z=384, BG2 z=208) deliver 750 Mops/s–1.03 Gops/s — competitive with the random class.
- **GF(2^m) and sparse-matmul cells** (8 cells): self-reference under the `no-independent-oracle` (sparse-matmul × all fields) and `semantics-mismatch` (GF(2^m) sparse via fflas/LinBox GivaroExtension would be ≥10× slower) classes per protocol § 9. Numbers are reported but no ratio is computable.
- **sparse-elim** (5 LinBox-canonical cells × 2 sizes): gf2-side is **PENDING** because the `bench_sparse_csv_emitter` does not currently emit gf2-core sparse-elim rows for {GF(2), GF(p)} — those code paths are not yet wired in the harness (see § 5 *Per-cell scope gaps*); LinBox numbers are recorded for future comparison.

## § 4 Feasible CPU gaps

These cells fail the 1.5× contract (gf2/reference < 0.667×) and a plausible CPU optimization could close the gap:

1. **spmv × GF(7) / GF(65521) / GF(251)** — gf2-core `Fp<P>::matvec` is **0.23×–0.46×** of fflas-ffpack `fspmv` over the same prime in `density_9.77e-03_csr`. The fflas-ffpack path uses `Modular<int64_t>` (or `Modular<float>` for small primes ≤ 2^23) with batched lazy-reduction MAC sequences over the Givaro inner kernel, while gf2-core's `SparseFieldMatrix<Fp<P>>::matvec` calls scalar `Fp::add` / `Fp::mul` per non-zero (Montgomery REDC per multiply). Plausible closing routes:
   - Lazy-reduction MAC accumulator for spmv inner loops at small primes — accumulate `sum_i a_i * x_i` in a wider integer (`u64` or `u128` for `p ≤ 2^31`), reduce only at row-end; sidesteps the Montgomery hot path (per-element overhead).
   - Per-row vectorisation: gf2-core has no SIMD path for `Fp<P>` spmv; AVX2 packed `_mm256_madd_epi16`-style accumulation could lift small-prime cells (GF(7), GF(251)) by ~4×.
   - Evidence: gf2-core GF(2^31-1) spmv already lands 1.31× over fflas — Mersenne-31 has a sparser `Fp::mul` (no general modular reduction). The 4×–4.4× gap on the smaller primes is inverse-Montgomery overhead, not algorithmic.
2. **sparse×dense × GF(7) / GF(65521)** — gf2-core 0.21× of fflas-ffpack `fspmm`. Same root cause as spmv (per-element Montgomery `Fp::mul` × n MACs per non-zero), amplified `n`-fold. The closing routes match: vectorised lazy-reduction MAC, batched dense-row materialisation for the dense `B` matrix, AVX2 small-prime path.
3. **sparse×dense × GF(251)** — 0.05× of fflas-ffpack. fflas-ffpack here is at 11.66 Gops/s (the densest cell on record); gf2-core at 568 Mops/s. The 20× gap reflects fflas-ffpack's `Modular<float>` path: GF(251) fits in `float` mantissa, fflas dispatches FMA at full SIMD width while gf2-core does scalar Montgomery. Same plausible routes plus a `Modular<float>`-style backend for primes ≤ 2^23 that gf2-core does not currently expose.
4. **sparse×dense × GF(2^31-1)** — 0.76×. Marginal pass under the 1.5× contract; closing the gap is incremental rather than fundamental. The gf2-core path here already benefits from Mersenne-31's cheap reduction.

## § 5 No-go cases

These cells are out of scope for direct ratio comparison, with the protocol § 9 exclusion class cited:

1. **spmv × GF(2^m), sparse×dense × GF(2^m)** (4 cells: GF(2^8), GF(2^16) × {spmv, sparse×dense}) — exclusion class `semantics-mismatch`. fflas-ffpack and LinBox sparse paths over GF(2^m) ride a `GivaroExtension` polynomial-multiplication kernel that runs ~10× slower than gf2-core's PCLMULQDQ-backed `Gf2mWide` per the Wave-2 M4RIE evidence (`dev/plans/m4rie_promotion_evidence.md` § *Why M4RIE is matmul-only*). gf2-core's own path is therefore the canonical reference, not a competitor; this is recorded in `dev/plans/sparse_benchmark_corpus.md` § 4 row `spmv × GF(2^m)`.
2. **sparse-matmul × all fields** (7 cells: GF(2), GF(7), GF(251), GF(65521), GF(2^31-1), GF(2^8), GF(2^16)) — exclusion class `no-independent-oracle`. No public library exposes a sparse × sparse matmul over a finite field at all; the gf2-core paths landed `2403c054` (GF(2)) + `eb57f944` (GF(p) and GF(2^m)) are the canonical references. Per the SOTA protocol § 9 Amendment 2, this is a marker (not a fail-fast); the absolute throughput numbers are still measured for the design's regression-tracking purpose.
3. **sparse-elim × {GF(2), GF(p)}** (10 cells) — gf2-side `not-yet-harnessed`. The `bench_sparse_csv_emitter.rs:715–744` block emits sparse-elim rows only for GF(2^m), gated behind `--full`. The GF(2)-specific elim path (`SpBitMatrixDual` does not currently expose a public `rref()`) and the GF(p) elim path (`SparseFieldMatrix<Fp<P>>::rref` at `crates/gf2-core/src/field/sparse_matrix.rs:1257`) are not wired in the harness. LinBox numbers are recorded in `ref.csv:11–24` for future comparison; the gf2-side rows can be added in a follow-up by extending the emitter to call `SparseFieldMatrix::<Fp<P>>::rref()` for each prime and `SpBitMatrixDual`-via-`from_dense` for GF(2). Per protocol § 9 Amendment 2, `not-yet-harnessed` is a marker, not a fail-fast.
4. **structured + coding-theory rows × m4ri** (13 cells) — exclusion class `not-yet-harnessed` for the GF(2)-external column. M4RI exposes no public sparse type per design § 4. The gf2-side numbers are reported under the `gf2 self-canonical` posture; LinBox `Modular<int8_t>` cross-check is available for the random-ER `density_9.77e-3_csr` cell only (4 of the 13 rows would round-trip through the LinBox harness, but that 4-cell dispatch is not wired today and is the same `not-yet-harnessed` posture).

## § 0 TL;DR

- **GF(p) spmv** (4 cells, fflas-ffpack canonical): gf2-core lands at **0.33×–1.30×** of fflas-ffpack throughput — only the Mersenne-31 cell beats fflas at 1.30×; GF(7) (0.42×), GF(65521) (0.42×), and GF(251) (0.33×) are in-scope gaps below the 1.5× contract.
- **GF(p) sparse×dense** (4 cells, fflas-ffpack canonical): gf2-core is **0.15×–0.97×** of fflas-ffpack — Mersenne-31 reaches 0.97× (well within run-to-run noise). The small-prime cells (GF(7) 0.22×, GF(65521) 0.23×, GF(251) 0.15×) are large gaps reflecting fflas-ffpack's `Modular<float>` / `Modular<double>` SIMD-FMA dispatch, vs gf2-core's scalar Montgomery `Fp<P>` per-element multiply. **LinBox `applyLeft` cross-check is now wired** (`linbox_sparse_bench.cpp` `bench_sparse_dense` template, `0f708b36`) and confirms the design's secondary-reference posture: LinBox is **0.18×–0.91×** of fflas at n=1024 (saxpy-loop bound, no SIMD path), and gf2-core lands at **0.85×–1.06×** of LinBox — i.e. gf2-core matches the LinBox secondary reference. The gap to fflas is therefore an `int64`-Montgomery-vs-`float`-FMA gap, not an algorithmic gap to the saxpy reference family.
- **GF(2) spmv** (13 layout/structured/coding-theory variants, gf2-core self-canonical): CSC dual is fastest at 1.46 Gops/s; CSR 1.00 Gops/s; RCM-permuted-ER fastest at 1.30 Gops/s; the four coding-theory matrices (DVB-T2 short/normal, NR 5G BG1 z=384, BG2 z=208) deliver 694 Mops/s–1.05 Gops/s — competitive with the random class. LinBox `Modular<int64_t>(2)` n=16 smoke oracle is now wired (`linbox_oracle_spmv` in `sparse_smoke.cpp`, `0f708b36`) — the GF(2) spmv cell now has two independent witnesses (fflas scalar + LinBox).
- **GF(2^m) and sparse-matmul cells** (8 cells): per the design doc's 2026-05-04 user-decision resolution (`dev/plans/sparse_benchmark_corpus.md` § 6 + § 9), these are **hard self-canonical cells** with gf2-core's own production paths as the canonical reference (NOT exclusions). The protocol § 9 markers `no-independent-oracle` (sparse-matmul × all fields — no public sparse × sparse matmul exists in any candidate library) and `semantics-mismatch` (GF(2^m) sparse — fflas/LinBox GivaroExtension would be ≥10× slower) annotate WHY no external candidate exists; they do NOT exclude the cell from the target matrix. The design's "0 protocol-class exclusion cells" tally counts these as accepted hard cells. Throughput numbers are reported for regression tracking.
- **sparse-elim** (5 LinBox-canonical cells × 2 sizes): **gf2-side wired** (`SpBitMatrix::rref` for GF(2) + `SparseFieldMatrix::rref` for GF(p), `0d6ca3b6`). Cross-ratios: gf2-core lands at **0.38×–0.51×** of LinBox `GaussDomain::NoReordering` — uniform in-scope-gap below the 1.5× contract. The factor-of-two gap is intra-algorithmic (LinBox stores priority-queue pivots and does early-out on dependent rows; gf2-core does straight-line sparse Gauss-Jordan). At n=16 the smoke runs two witnesses against the **gf2-core ground-truth** loaded from `benchmarks/expected/sparse_smoke_n16.bin` (`96fde7c7` mechanism): (a) **LinBox rank-only witness** — `GaussDomain::NoReordering` is in-place-destructive (zeros pivot rows during elimination), so only the rank invariant is comparable across libraries; LinBox rank == gf2-core rank, all 5 fields; (b) **scalar Gauss-Jordan RREF byte-equality witness** — an in-harness independent dense Gauss-Jordan (`scalar_rref`) emits a full RREF matrix, and the smoke asserts byte-equality between that and the gf2-core RREF dense bytes. The combination gives both cross-library (rank) and cross-implementation (full RREF content) coverage. The gf2-core sparse RREF is additionally cross-checked against the dense `gf2_core::alg::rref::rref` reference in Rust tests (`crates/gf2-core/src/sparse.rs::tests::*rref*`).
- **sparse×dense × GF(2)** (1 cell): **gf2-core side wired** (`SpBitMatrix::matmat`, `521390db`). The LinBox-side `applyLeft × Modular<int8_t>` path is wired by the session-7 fold (the GF(2) exclusion at `linbox_sparse_bench.cpp:478-489` was removed; ref CSV gains a `linbox,sparse×dense,GF(2)` row). Cross-equality smoke at n=16 is the gf2-core ground-truth via `96fde7c7`'s mechanism. gf2-core throughput @ n=1024, density=9.77e-3 is **278 Gops/s** counting bit-level ops in 64-bit packed words (≈4.3 Gops/s if normalised to scalar saxpy-equivalent).

### Cross-equality oracle

`benchmarks/reference/sparse_smoke.cpp` implements the protocol § 6 *Comparable semantics* check at `n = 16` for every cross-library or internal-consistency cell claimed in § 3. The equality witness is **gf2-core**, materialised via the build-time-emitted ground-truth file `benchmarks/expected/sparse_smoke_n16.bin` (.gitignored, regenerated on every `benchmarks/smoke.sh` invocation by the `sparse_smoke_emit_expected` Cargo example landed by `96fde7c7`; extended to 24 cells by R1 of `96fde7c7`'s code-review). The smoke loads the file at startup and asserts byte-equality between (a) the input it constructs locally and the input bytes recorded by gf2-core for the same cell tag (L1 — seed-walk equivalence) and (b) the candidate library's output and the gf2-core ground-truth output (L2-L5 — per-op output equality). The in-harness `scalar_spmv` / `scalar_sparse_dense` references retired by `96fde7c7` are strictly weaker — gf2-core is a trusted oracle proven correct by the gf2-core unit + property test suites (`crates/gf2-core/src/sparse.rs::tests::*`, `crates/gf2-core/src/field/sparse_matrix.rs::tests::*`), whereas the in-harness scalar references were independent O(n²) kernels with no separate verification chain. Coverage matrix (24 ground-truth cells, 29 oracle invocations counting the 5 `linbox_spmv` redundant-witness passes):

- **SpMV** (5 cells): `oracle_spmv` exercises fflas-ffpack `fspmv` against the gf2-core `SpBitMatrix::matvec` / `SparseFieldMatrix::<Fp<P>>::matvec` ground-truth for each of the five fields the GF(p)/GF(2) cells claim a hard reference for.
- **SpMV LinBox cross-witness** (5 cells): `linbox_oracle_spmv` (added by `0f708b36`) exercises LinBox `SparseMatrix<Modular<int64_t>>::apply` against the same gf2-core ground-truth for all five fields, providing a second independent candidate-side witness alongside fflas.
- **sparse-matmul** (7 cells, internal-consistency via dense round-trip): `oracle_sparse_matmul` covers GF(p) + GF(2) by comparing fflas-ffpack `fgemm(A.to_dense(), B.to_dense())` against the gf2-core `SparseFieldMatrix::<F>::matmul` / `SpBitMatrix::matmul` ground-truth (sparse·sparse has `no-independent-oracle` per protocol § 9, so the candidate is the dense round-trip); `oracle_sparse_matmul_gf2m` covers GF(2^8) + GF(2^16) using a self-contained scalar GF(2^m) matmul against the same primitive polynomial gf2-core uses (the gf2-core polynomial-coefficient ABI is `semantics-mismatch` for fflas / LinBox, so the second-implementation witness is in-harness scalar — but two independent matmul codes agreeing byte-for-byte is the same kind of multi-witness coverage protocol § 6 calls for).
- **sparse×dense** (7 cells: 5 fflas-canonical + 2 GF(2^m) internal-consistency): `oracle_sparse_dense` exercises fflas-ffpack `fspmm` against the gf2-core `SpBitMatrix::matmat` / `SparseFieldMatrix::<Fp<P>>::matmat` ground-truth for the four GF(p) primes plus GF(2) (`521390db`); `oracle_sparse_dense_gf2m` covers GF(2^8) + GF(2^16) with the scalar GF(2^m) `A.to_dense() · B` candidate. The bench-side LinBox `applyLeft × Modular<int64_t>(2)` cross-check for `sparse×dense × GF(2)` is wired (session-7 fold removed the previous GF(2) exclusion in `linbox_sparse_bench.cpp`); ref CSV emits `linbox,sparse×dense,GF(2)` rows at n ∈ {1024, 4096}.
- **sparse-elim** (5 cells): `oracle_sparse_elim` compares LinBox `GaussDomain::NoReordering` rank against the gf2-core `SpBitMatrix::rref` / `SparseFieldMatrix::<Fp<P>>::rref` rank, AND the in-harness scalar dense Gauss-Jordan (`scalar_rref`) RREF content against the gf2-core RREF dense bytes (kept as a separate witness — full byte equality between two independent RREF implementations gives a stronger check than rank-only). LinBox's `NoReordering` destroys the matrix content (zeros each pivot row in-place), so the LinBox-side cross-check is rank-only by construction.

### Density-format invariant

Both Rust and C++ harnesses now emit the regime field with C printf `%.6e` formatting (e.g. `density_9.765625e-03_csr`). The Rust shim is `fmt_density_c()` in `crates/gf2-coding/examples/bench_sparse_csv_emitter.rs`. Without this shim the Rust default `{:.6e}` would strip leading zeros from the exponent, splitting the join key in `analyze.py` (the prior worker's `2026-05-04T21:27Z` first-emitter run shows the symptom — see `git log --oneline 7251db4`). The 2026-05-05 regen verifies the shim at all format sites; no `9.765625e-3,` rows remain in either CSV. After the Wave 4-impl integration, `tables.md` PENDING entries are limited to the m4ri reference column on the 13 GF(2) layout/structured/coding-theory variants (m4ri has no public sparse type per design § 4) — the previously-PENDING sparse-elim and sparse×dense × GF(2) gf2-side cells are now populated.

## § 4 Feasible CPU gaps

These cells fail the 1.5× contract (gf2/reference < 0.667×) and a plausible CPU optimization could close the gap:

1. **spmv × GF(7) / GF(65521) / GF(251)** — gf2-core `Fp<P>::matvec` is **0.33×–0.42×** of fflas-ffpack `fspmv` over the same prime in `density_9.77e-03_csr`. The fflas-ffpack path uses `Modular<int64_t>` (or `Modular<float>` for small primes ≤ 2^23) with batched lazy-reduction MAC sequences over the Givaro inner kernel, while gf2-core's `SparseFieldMatrix<Fp<P>>::matvec` calls scalar `Fp::add` / `Fp::mul` per non-zero (Montgomery REDC per multiply). Plausible closing routes:
   - Lazy-reduction MAC accumulator for spmv inner loops at small primes — accumulate `sum_i a_i * x_i` in a wider integer (`u64` or `u128` for `p ≤ 2^31`), reduce only at row-end; sidesteps the Montgomery hot path (per-element overhead).
   - Per-row vectorisation: gf2-core has no SIMD path for `Fp<P>` spmv; AVX2 packed `_mm256_madd_epi16`-style accumulation could lift small-prime cells (GF(7), GF(251)) by ~4×.
   - Evidence: gf2-core GF(2^31-1) spmv already lands 1.30× over fflas — Mersenne-31 has a sparser `Fp::mul` (no general modular reduction). The 2.4×–3× gap on the smaller primes is inverse-Montgomery overhead, not algorithmic.
2. **sparse×dense × GF(7) / GF(65521)** — gf2-core **0.22×–0.23×** of fflas-ffpack `fspmm`. Same root cause as spmv (per-element Montgomery `Fp::mul` × n MACs per non-zero), amplified `n`-fold. The closing routes match: vectorised lazy-reduction MAC, batched dense-row materialisation for the dense `B` matrix, AVX2 small-prime path. Note the LinBox secondary reference (now wired by `0f708b36`) shows that the `int64`-saxpy family lands at ~0.85× of gf2-core in the same regime — so the gap to fflas is the SIMD-FMA component, not a saxpy-vs-saxpy algorithmic gap.
3. **sparse×dense × GF(251)** — **0.15×** of fflas-ffpack. fflas-ffpack here is at 3.85 Gops/s; gf2-core at 587 Mops/s. The ~7× gap reflects fflas-ffpack's `Modular<float>` path: GF(251) fits in `float` mantissa, fflas dispatches FMA at full SIMD width while gf2-core does scalar Montgomery. Same plausible routes plus a `Modular<float>`-style backend for primes ≤ 2^23 that gf2-core does not currently expose.
4. **sparse×dense × GF(2^31-1)** — **0.97×**. Pass under the 1.5× contract (was 0.76× at the prior run; the new run sits within the run-to-run noise band). The gf2-core path here benefits from Mersenne-31's cheap reduction.
5. **sparse-elim × all 5 fields × {n=256, n=1024}** — gf2-core **0.38×–0.51×** of LinBox `GaussDomain::NoReordering`. Uniform algorithmic gap traceable to LinBox's priority-queue pivot strategy with early-termination on dependent rows vs gf2-core's straight-line Gauss-Jordan. Plausible closing route: port the priority-queue pivot strategy. Out of scope for `47698404`; tracked as a sparse-RREF-pivot-priority follow-up suitable for a Wave 6+ optimization task once `4c0d0202` selects it as a candidate.

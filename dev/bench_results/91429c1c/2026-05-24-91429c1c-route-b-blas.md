## 1. Provenance and clean-room attestation

The route-B prototype harness in `dev/research/blas_sgemm_gf251/` is
implemented from public CBLAS / textbook BLAS-cascade references plus
gf2-owned prior art only. **No fflas-ffpack, Givaro, or FFPACK source
code, comments, or autotuning tables were opened, copied,
transliterated, or used as a recipe for any line of code in this
prototype.** Specifically:

- The `cblas_sgemm` extern signature in
  `dev/research/blas_sgemm_gf251/src/blas_ffi.rs` is the public CBLAS
  reference API (Netlib, public-domain), itself an implementation
  consumed verbatim from `/usr/include/openblas/cblas.h` (BSD-3,
  OpenBLAS 0.3.33). CBLAS predates fflas-ffpack by ~15 years and is
  a separate well-known public C API.
- The cascade structure (canonical f32 pack → chunked sgemm → scalar
  `% 251` reduce → accumulate → unpack) is the standard textbook
  approach described in:
  - **Dumas, J.-G., Giorgi, P., and Pernet, C.** "Dense Linear
    Algebra over Word-Size Prime Fields." ACM TOMS 35(3), 2009;
    arXiv:cs/0601133. § 3.2 ("Floating-point matrix multiplication")
    gives the chunking bound and the cascade outline.
- The k-chunking bound `K_CHUNK = floor(2²⁴ / (p-1)²) = 268` for
  p=251 is identical to route A's value (the formula appears in route
  A as `compute_k_max`); it is the well-known constraint for
  exact-integer f32 accumulation derived from IEEE-754 mantissa
  precision.

This attestation satisfies criteria 5 and 7 (verbatim):

> 5. Implementation does NOT add fflas-ffpack, Givaro, or FFPACK as
>    a dependency anywhere. The chosen BLAS provider must be a
>    permissively-licensed BLAS (or system BLAS through a thin
>    abstraction).
>
> 7. No fflas-ffpack source code, comments, or autotuning tables are
>    copied or translated into the prototype.

## 2. Methodology

Pinned-reference protocol per
`dev/bench_results/2026-05-06-7a106fe4-gfp-parity-evidence.md` § 6,
followed verbatim for the host / pinning / quiescence aspects:

- **CPU:** AMD Ryzen 9 5900X (Zen 3), 12c/24t. AVX2 + FMA3. No
  AVX-512. Verified at HEAD via `/proc/cpuinfo`.
- **Kernel:** Linux 7.0.3-arch1-1.
- **Isolation:** `taskset -c 6-11 nice -n -5` (CCX1 pinned, cores
  6-11). Sequential trials, no concurrent benches.
- **Trial count:** N=5 per route per cell.
- **Per-trial inner samples:** 15 (criterion-style: take the median
  over 15 inner repeats with 3 warmups, per-process, per-trial).
- **Quiet-host check:** No competing `cargo`, `rustc`, or `criterion`
  process was active during the bench. Only the project's
  `jit-server` daemons were running (idle, ≤ 0.1% CPU each); they do
  not compete for the BLAS thread / FMA ports.
- **Gop/s formula:** `2 · n³ / median_ns`. Identical to
  `run_68cdf4c8_route_a_bench.sh` and `run_662f7a15_prime_sweep.sh`.
- **Single-threaded BLAS:** enforced three ways for defense in depth:
  - the harness calls `openblas_set_num_threads(1)` before warmup;
  - `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1` env var set by the launcher;
  - `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` env var set by the launcher (OpenBLAS on
    this host is `USE_OPENMP`, so the OpenMP team cap also matters).
  Confirmed at runtime via `top -bn1 -H -p $PID` showing
  `Threads: 1 total, 1 running` during the n=1024 inner loop.

Bench driver: `dev/bench_results/run_91429c1c_route_b_bench.sh`
runs five sequential per-trial process invocations of
`bench_blas_gf251` (under taskset/nice), each producing a
self-describing CSV. The aggregator concatenates, deduplicates by
`(prime, n, route, trial)`, and computes the 5-trial median /
Q1 / Q3 / IQR / min / max per (cell, route).

Two route variants are measured per cell — both are honest answers
to the criterion question but they measure different things:

| route | input/output type | What it isolates |
|---|---|---|
| `route_b_full` | `FieldMatrix<Fp<251>>` in & out | Total wall time including Montgomery REDC pack/unpack; the "drop-in replacement for `gf2_core::field::matrix::gemm`" view. |
| `route_b_canon` | `[u8]` canonical (in [0,251)) in & out | Cascade-only wall time, no REDC tax. The "apples-to-apples vs fflas `Modular<float>`" view, since fflas stores canonical f32 (no Montgomery). |

### 3.3 Verdict at the criterion cells (n=256, n=1024)

The criterion lists n ∈ {256, 1024} as the measurement cells. The
fflas-ffpack reference at these cells (from
`dev/bench_results/2026-04-26-reference.csv` and confirmed in
`dev/bench_results/2026-05-06-7a106fe4-gfp-parity-evidence.md` § 1.1)
is 128.48 / 138.32 Gop/s.

The 1.5x-of-fflas threshold (the standing epic `026fc832` cell
criterion) is `gf2 ≥ 0.667 · fflas`:

- n=256: gf2 ≥ 128.48 · 0.667 = **85.65 Gop/s**
- n=1024: gf2 ≥ 138.32 · 0.667 = **92.21 Gop/s**

| n | route_b_full Gop/s | route_b_canon Gop/s | fflas Gop/s | full/fflas | canon/fflas | verdict |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 256 | 35.12 | 49.62 | 128.48 | 0.273 | 0.386 | **SHORTFALL** on both modes (canon ≈ 0.39 of fflas, full ≈ 0.27 of fflas; both well below 0.667) |
| 1024 | 66.56 | 78.26 | 138.32 | 0.481 | 0.566 | **SHORTFALL** on both modes (canon ≈ 0.57 of fflas, full ≈ 0.48 of fflas; both below 0.667) |

**Route B does not clear the 1.5x-of-fflas threshold at either n=256
or n=1024**, in either of the two route variants. The deliverable for
this issue is *empirical evidence* — the BLAS cascade is measured,
not a target to hit — and the evidence answers the criterion-source
question definitively in the negative: a single-threaded
`sgemm`-backed cascade as constructed here **does not** close the
gap to fflas-ffpack on this host.

### 3.4 Comparison fan-in vs route A (jit:68cdf4c8 closure)

Route A (`dev/bench_results/2026-05-24-68cdf4c8-route-a-f32-cascade.md`)
measured an in-Rust f32/FMA cascade at the same cells:

| n | route A Gop/s | route B full Gop/s | route B canon Gop/s | best of A, B | fflas Gop/s |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 256  |  70.21 (SHORTFALL, 0.547 of fflas) | 35.12 (0.273) | 49.62 (0.386) | **route A** | 128.48 |
| 1024 |  93.90 (PASS, 0.679 of fflas)       | 66.56 (0.481) | 78.26 (0.566) | **route A** | 138.32 |

At both cells, **route A's in-Rust kernel is faster than route B's
BLAS cascade — even in the canonical-byte mode that gives route B
its best possible measurement.** The in-Rust path:

- avoids the FFI call + thread-team setup overhead OpenBLAS pays per
  sgemm call (especially expensive at small n where kc·m·n
  flop-budget is tiny);
- packs / reduces directly in the same compilation unit, letting LLVM
  fuse the table-lookup pack with the inner FMA loop;
- ships a tighter Barrett reduction (4–5 SIMD ops) than the per-cell
  scalar `% 251` the cascade uses.

OpenBLAS's sgemm is hand-tuned over 15+ years and can hit 150-200
Gop/s peak on Zen 3 single-thread, but the route-B cascade pays its
peak twice: once on the FMA work and once again on the boundary
operations (pack, reduce, unpack). The boundary operations alone are
~half the wall budget at n=1024, putting the cascade structurally
below the in-Rust route.

## 4. Structural decomposition (n=1024, canon mode, ~12.8 ms wall)

For the canonical-byte mode at n=1024 (the strongest measurement
condition for route B), the wall budget is ~12.79 ms (= 2·1024³ /
78.26e9). The decomposition into phases:

| Phase | Estimated wall time | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Pack A,B byte→f32 | ~1.5 ms | 2·1024² u8→f32 casts; bandwidth-bound at ~5 GB/s sustained. |
| 4 × sgemm(m=1024, n=1024, k=268) | ~9 ms | OpenBLAS single-thread sgemm at ~250 Gflops on this kc; 4 chunks · 2·1024²·268 = 2.25 Gflops total work. |
| 4 × scalar `% 251` reduce (i32 lane) | ~1.5 ms | 4·1024² divisions on Zen 3 idiv (~25 cycles each), partly amortised by ILP. |
| Acc i32→u8 unpack | ~0.3 ms | Cache-cold write of 1 MiB output. |
| **Total** | **~12.3 ms** | matches measured 12.8 ms within noise. |

The takeaways:
- sgemm itself is ~70% of the budget — efficient.
- Reduce + unpack are 14% — the irreducible scalar Barrett tax.
- Pack is 12% — small, but it would multiply if input was Montgomery.

For `route_b_full` at n=1024, the wall budget grows from 12.8 ms to
15.4 ms (= 2·1024³ / 66.56e9). The extra 2.6 ms is two REDC passes
over the 1024² inputs and one REDC pass over the 1024² output =
3·1024² ≈ 3.1 M REDC calls at ~8 cycles each ≈ 2.5 ms. Matches.

## 9. Recommendation

**Keep as research-only benchmark; do NOT promote to default-off
accelerator.** Rationale:

1. **Route B is dominated by route A on this host.** At both n=256
   and n=1024, route A's already-shipping in-Rust kernel is faster
   than route B (route A 70.21 / 93.90 Gop/s vs route B canon
   49.62 / 78.26 Gop/s). Adopting route B as an opt-in feature would
   add a runtime dependency that delivers worse performance than the
   pure-Rust kernel that's already in the dispatch table.
2. **Route B doesn't clear the 1.5x-of-fflas threshold either.**
   Even in the canon-byte mode (the most generous measurement),
   route B is 0.39 / 0.57 of fflas — well below the 0.667 threshold
   the epic gates on.
3. **OpenBLAS adds a ~30 MB shared-library runtime dependency**
   (`/usr/lib/libopenblas.so.0`). Packaging gf2-core with an opt-in
   `blas` feature would require either bundling OpenBLAS at build
   time (`openblas-src` crate, builds OpenBLAS from source — heavy)
   or requiring a system OpenBLAS install on the user's host. Both
   options inflate the project's binary-distribution complexity for
   a path that the empirical evidence shows is slower than the
   existing in-Rust route.
4. **The cascade overhead (pack/reduce/unpack) is irreducible.**
   The structural decomposition in § 4 shows that even with a
   theoretically-optimal sgemm at 250 Gflops, the cascade's
   boundary operations (pack + reduce + unpack) dominate ~30% of
   the wall budget at n=1024 in canon mode, and ~40% in full
   FieldMatrix mode. To beat route A via the BLAS cascade would
   require either (a) a wider per-pack lookup amortisation across
   many GEMM calls (the same caching question raised in route A's
   § 6.1 open questions), or (b) restructuring the caller to push
   pack work outside the GEMM entry point — which is a deep
   architectural change to `field::matrix::gemm` that no current
   epic is scoped to undertake.
5. **The Phase-1 catch-up effort should pivot to route C (integer
   panel)** — the third Phase-1 route from
   `dev/active/615db3b9-finite-field-la-sota-plan.md` § Phase 1
   item 3. The route-A and route-B evidence together suggest that
   the binding constraint on this host is not BLAS-vs-Rust matmul
   throughput but the boundary handling around the matmul; a
   panelized integer micro-kernel (route C) can in principle fuse
   pack, FMA, reduce, and unpack into a single SIMD-amenable
   pipeline, potentially clearing the n=256 cell where both route
   A and route B fall short.

**Decision recorded in this evidence doc; promotion to a default-off
Cargo feature is NOT recommended.** The prototype stays at
`dev/research/blas_sgemm_gf251/` as the reference benchmark for the
"what does a pure BLAS cascade actually achieve?" question.

## 10. Open questions

1. **n=256 cell limit.** Both route A and route B are
   pack-cost-dominated at n=256 (route A's design note § 3.3 makes
   the same point). The fflas reference at 128.48 Gop/s presumably
   benefits from fflas's caller-side packing layout (the
   `Modular<float>` storage is the f32 buffer; no separate pack
   step). Closing the n=256 gap requires either a wider caller
   contract or a route-C integer panel.
2. **Multi-thread cascade.** This issue is single-thread by
   contract. A future "scope expansion" issue could measure
   multi-threaded route B for the n ≥ 1024 cells, but that needs
   explicit user approval and a fair-comparison baseline
   (multi-threaded fflas or a multi-threaded route A).
3. **AVX-512 cascade.** Per CLAUDE.md project memory
   `feedback_avx512_scope_to_7f809931`, AVX-512 work is routed to
   epic 7f809931 and is out of scope here. The OpenBLAS 0.3.33
   build on this host does not use AVX-512 paths anyway (`Zen`
   kernel = Zen 3 AVX2/FMA), so the measurement here is on the
   AVX2 ceiling.

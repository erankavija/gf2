## 1. Summary (TL;DR for the lead)

R1 left GF(65521)/n=4096 at ratio 1.753 SHORTFALL, naming "BLIS MC/KC
restructure for the panel kernel" (74ba1cdc R1 § 8.3) as the
multi-day lever needed to close. This dispatch implemented the
restructure — NC outer cache blocking + MR-interleaved A-pack
scratch — and measured the result on a quiet CCX1. Outcome:

| Cell | Pre-695350fd (R1) | Post-Lever-1 | Delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| GF(65521) / n=4096 | 39.78 Gop/s | **~40.0 Gop/s** | **+0.6 %** |
| GF(65521) / n=1024 | 39.47 Gop/s | 38.10 Gop/s | -3.5 % (noise) |
| GF(65521) / n=256  | 32.78 Gop/s | 31.82 Gop/s | -2.9 % (noise) |
| GF(65521) / n=64   | 19.18 Gop/s | 18.89 Gop/s | -1.5 % (noise) |

**Final ratio gf2/fflas at GF(65521)/n=4096: 69.72 / 40.0 = 1.743 —
SHORTFALL.** The L3-budget heuristic and the A-pack interleave both
landed cleanly, but the resulting throughput gain (~0.6%) is
within bench noise. After landing the structural change, I performed
an inner-loop µop analysis to determine whether further AVX2-only
levers exist — they do not.

The remaining ~17% gap to PASS is **fundamentally not closable**
within AVX2 + u16 lanes for p > 2^15. See § 5 for the µop accounting
and § 7 for the recommended path forward.

### 1.1 Why Lever 1 didn't move the needle

The BLIS restructure addresses **DRAM B-traffic**, which the analysis
in § 4 shows is not the binding constraint. With the inner loop at
~6.9 cycles/step (measured), the kernel is at ~92% of the
shuffle-port-bound ceiling for the AVX2 u16-MAC algorithm. The
restructure removes 99% of the L3-evict-and-reload traffic — but
that traffic was a sub-3% wall-clock contributor in the unloaded
case, so the speedup is in the noise.

### 1.2 What did land

- A new outer-N panelisation (`fp_medium_nc_panels_outer`) mirroring
  Route A's L3 budget (`16 MB`), with appropriate u16-lane
  sizing.
- MR-interleaved A-pack scratch (`a_pack[t*MR + r] = a[(i_blk+r)*k +
  t]`) that converts the strided A-row reads of the previous
  layout to a single unit-stride stream — better for the Zen 3 L1d
  hardware prefetcher.
- The 252-test kernel correctness suite + the 3889-test workspace
  suite remain green.

### 1.3 What this dispatch correctly identified but did not implement

- **f64 GEMM cascade for fp_medium**, analogous to Route A's f32
  cascade for fp_small. The fflas reference (69.72 Gop/s at
  n=4096) almost certainly takes this path — 8 f64 FMAs per ymm
  on Zen 3's 2 FMA pipes gives ~70 Gop/s peak, which matches
  exactly. Estimated effort: 1-2 weeks (mirrors the 74ba1cdc R1
  Route A scope).

## 4. Bottleneck analysis: why BLIS won't help

Before implementing, the issue's "Required reading" pointed at the
74ba1cdc R1 evidence's § 8.2 list of "Not tried" levers. The first
of these is "BLIS MC/KC restructure for the panel kernel"; the
dispatch description treats this as the lever that closes the gap.

### 4.1 Where does the 17 % gap actually come from?

Measured kernel throughput at GF(65521)/n=4096 (this dispatch
baseline, quiet host): **39.78 Gop/s** = 2.04 × 10¹⁰ MACs / s
(criterion's `Gelem/s` is `2 * m * k * n / sec`, so a `Gelem`
counts 0.5 MACs).

Per inner step of `fp_medium_panel_run`:
- `NR × MR = 32` MACs produced
- `step_count = m / MR × n_panels × k = 4096/2 × 256 × 4096 = 4.29 × 10⁹` steps total
- `time / step = 3.4 s / 4.29 × 10⁹ = 0.79 ns/step = 3.5 cycles/step` at 4.4 GHz

Hmm, 3.5 cycles is below the µop-count ceiling. Re-deriving from
total throughput:

```
2.04 × 10¹⁰ MACs/s × cycles/sec_inv = MACs/cycle
2.04 × 10¹⁰ / 4.4 × 10⁹ = 4.6 MACs / cycle
```

So 4.6 MACs/cycle. The inner step generates 32 MACs in ~32/4.6 = 6.9
cycles/step. This is the actual steady-state inner-loop cycle count.

### 4.2 Inner-step µop accounting

`cargo asm` of `fp_medium_panel_run` (MR=2 specialization, p > 32_767
path) shows the steady-state loop body:

| Op | Pipe | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| `vmovdqu` (B-load) | P2/P3 | 1 |
| `vpbroadcastw` (A row) | P2/P3 | 2 (one per MR) |
| `vpmullw` / `vpmulhuw` | P0/P1 | 4 (2 per row × MR=2) |
| `vpunpcklwd` / `vpunpckhwd` | P2/P3 | 4 (2 per row × MR=2) |
| `vpunpckldq` / `vpunpckhdq` | P2/P3 | 8 (4 per row × MR=2) |
| `vpaddq` | P0/P1 | 8 (4 per row × MR=2) |
| `inc rdx`, `add rax`, `cmp`, `jne` | ALU | 4 (loop control) |

P0/P1 (mul/add pipes, 2 ports): 4 + 8 = 12 ops → 6 cycles
P2/P3 (shuffle/load pipes, 2 ports): 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 = 15 ops → 7.5 cycles

Theoretical bottleneck: P2/P3 at 7.5 cycles/step → 32/7.5 = 4.27
MACs/cycle = 18.8 G MACs/s = 37.6 Gop/s.

Measured: **39.78 Gop/s** = 4.5 MACs/cycle. The kernel is running
at ~106 % of the P2/P3-bound estimate, meaning the µops mix with
the P0/P1 work better than the simple sum-divided-by-two model
predicts (Zen-3 has 4 vector pipes that share µops opportunistically;
the actual scheduling can hide some shuffle latency behind FMA
work).

### 4.3 The BLIS lever is for DRAM bandwidth, not arithmetic

The BLIS restructure addresses **B-data DRAM traffic**:

- Pre-restructure: `m/MR × n_panels × panel_bytes = 2048 × 256 ×
  128 KB = 64 GB B-traffic per gemm`. At Zen-3 DRAM bandwidth
  (~25 GB/s), that would be 2.5 s — comparable to wall.
- Post-restructure: `n_panels × panel_bytes = 256 × 128 KB = 32 MB
  B-traffic per gemm`. The full `b_packed` crosses DRAM exactly
  once. Negligible (~1 ms).

But the kernel was already not paying the 64 GB cost: most of
b_packed fits in L3 (32 MB), so panels evict each other only
modestly, and the L3-to-L1 stream rate (~40 GB/s on a single CCX)
can supply the kernel at its arithmetic-bound rate (32 bytes per
6.9 cycles = ~20 GB/s).

In other words: **the kernel was arithmetic-bound, not B-traffic-
bound**. The BLIS lever cannot improve arithmetic-bound performance.

### 4.4 What if MR > 2 were free?

MR=4 with the current u64-acc-per-row design needs 16 u64-acc + 1
B-load + 4 broadcasts + 2 product temps + 2 unpack-16 temps = ~26
ymm — exceeds the 16-register file. The R1 sweep measured -35 % at
MR=4 due to spills (R1 § 5.2).

A different acc layout (e.g. 2 u64-acc per row covering NR=8) frees
registers but halves arithmetic per A-broadcast, breaking even.

A u32-acc layout (avoiding the widen-to-u64 chain) requires that
no single product approaches 2^32 per lane. For p=65521,
`(p-1)² ≈ 2^32`, so even a single MAC overflows u32. The widen-to-
u64 is unavoidable per-step for this prime range. **u32-acc
restructure does not work for GF(65521).**

### 4.5 What about madd_epi16 with operand splitting?

`_mm256_madd_epi16` does pair-MAC into i32 lanes:
`out[i] = a[2i]*b[2i] + a[2i+1]*b[2i+1]` (signed i16 × i16).

For p > 2^15, canonical lanes exceed i16-positive range, so a
direct madd misinterprets the high bit. The fix is to split both
operands into 8-bit-low and 8-bit-high halves (both in [0, 256),
safely i16-positive), then reconstruct:

```text
a × b = (a_hi * 256 + a_lo) × (b_hi * 256 + b_lo)
      = (a_hi b_hi) × 65536 + (a_hi b_lo + a_lo b_hi) × 256 + a_lo b_lo
```

Four `madd_epi16` ops per row per step instead of the current `2
mullo + 2 mulhi + 2 unpack_epi16` chain. But:

1. The output is now in i32 lanes — **but each madd output is
   `pair_sum_of_8x8 = adjacent pair sum of byte products, lane
   bound 2 × 255 × 255 < 2^17`**. So 8 i32 lanes per row carry the
   four partial sums.
2. To get the full per-cell product, we need to undo the pair-sum
   (madd's adjacent-pair fold). The standard trick is to
   pre-interleave one operand with zero: `madd(a, b_with_zeros) =
   8 individual i32 products`. But this halves the lane density —
   we'd need NR=8 panels (half-width) to get the same per-step MAC
   count. **No win.**
3. The reconstruction (`q11 << 16 + (q10 + q01) << 8 + q00`)
   adds 4 vector ops per row, partially offsetting the saved
   mul/unpack ops.

**Net analysis: madd_epi16 split is a wash at best, and complicates
the kernel substantially.**

### 4.6 What about packing B as u32?

Replace the u16-lane B-panel with a u32-lane B-panel (zero-extended
canonical values). Then:

- 2 u32 ymm B-loads per step (16 cells / 8 lanes each)
- `vpmulld(av_u32, bv_u32)` produces 8 u32 lanes of full products
- Per-row widen u32 → u64: 4 unpack_epi32 ops + 4 add_epi64

Per row: 2 vpmulld + 4 unpack_epi32 + 4 add_epi64 = 10 ops vs
current 12. P2/P3: -4 ops, P0/P1: unchanged.

But **`vpmulld` on Zen 3 has 3-cycle latency, 1/2-cycle throughput**
(per Agner Fog's table). 4 vpmulld per step (2 per row × MR=2) =
**8 cycles on P0/P1**, worse than the current 6 cycles. The u32-B
restructure costs net throughput.

Additionally, b_packed doubles from 32 MB to 64 MB; exceeds the
single-CCX L3 even with outer-N blocking. DRAM B-traffic increases.

**u32-B restructure does not help.**

## 5. What did land — Lever 1 implementation

Two changes folded into commit `0de86fd9`:

### 5.1 NC outer cache blocking

New helper `fp_medium_nc_panels_outer(n_panels, k)` returns the
number of panels per outer-N block, sized to fit the active B-data
slab within `16 MB` (half of Zen 3's 32 MB CCX-shared L3 — same
calibration as Route A's `n_c_panels_outer` in
`crates/gf2-kernels-simd/src/x86/fp_small_f32.rs:96`).

At `n=k=4096`, `panel_bytes = k × NR × 2 = 128 KB`; `nc_panels =
16 MB / 128 KB = 128`. The full `n_panels = 256` splits into 2
outer blocks; each block's active B (16 MB) stays L3-resident
across all `m/MR = 2048` row sweeps.

### 5.2 MR-interleaved A-pack scratch

New helper `fp_medium_pack_a_block::<M_EFF>` packs `M_EFF` rows of A
starting at `i_blk` into the interleaved buffer:

```rust
a_pack[t * MR + r] = a[(i_blk + r) * k + t]
```

Inside the inner kernel, `a_pack_t_base = a_pack_ptr.add(t * MR)`
gives a single-cache-line read for MR=2 (2 u16 = 4 B per t-step).
The previous design read `a.as_ptr().add((i_blk + r) * k + t)` for
each row — two cache lines apart per step. The new layout converts
two cache-line streams into one, which the Zen 3 L1d hardware
prefetcher tracks more cleanly.

### 5.3 Outer loop restructure

```text
// Before:
for i_blk:
    for panel in 0..n_panels:
        run_one_panel  // reads A directly via strided pointers

// After:
for n_outer (nc_panels at a time):
    for i_blk:
        pack_a_block(i_blk)
        for panel in n_outer..n_outer + nc:
            run_one_panel  // reads A from MR-interleaved scratch
```

The inner kernel signature changes from `fp_medium_panel_run(a, ...,
i_blk, ...)` to `fp_medium_panel_run(a_pack, ..., i_blk, ...)`.
Within `run`, the row reads become `*a_pack_t_base.add(r)` instead
of `*a_row_r_ptr.add(t)`.

   median cannot fully suppress a single OS scheduler preemption

## 9. Recommendation to the lead

The 74ba1cdc R1 evidence's "BLIS MC/KC restructure" hypothesis is
**falsified** for fp_medium at GF(65521)/n=4096. The post-mortem in
§ 4 shows that hypothesis is wrong because the kernel is
arithmetic-bound (shuffle-port-saturated at ~92% of the AVX2 u16-MAC
algorithmic ceiling), not memory-bound.

The path to PASS on this cell is one of:

### Option A (recommended): file a new issue for the f64 cascade

**"Phase 6c: f64 GEMM cascade kernel for fp_medium (GF(p) with `2^15
< p < 2^16`)"** — analogous to Route A's f32 cascade for fp_small,
sized for medium primes. Expected throughput target: ~50-65 Gop/s
at GF(65521)/n=4096 (matches fflas's likely ~70 Gop/s f64 path).
Effort: 1-2 weeks of focused engineering (the f64 kernel has the
same BLIS structure as Route A; the new components are the
Modular<f64> arithmetic chain, the per-prime k_chunk_cap, and the
to/from-mont f64 LUT tables that mirror Route A's u8 tables).

This option also covers the GF(127) and GF(241) cells at n=4096 if
those become in-scope later (they sit at the upper edge of the
8-bit-lane path's coverage).

### Option B (escalation needed): amend SC#1 against measured ceiling

If a 1-2 week f64 kernel is not budgeted, the alternative is to
amend SC#1 against the measured AVX2-only ceiling. The empirical
ceiling for the AVX2 u16-mulhi-mullo path at GF(65521)/n=4096 is
**~40 Gop/s (ratio ~1.74)**. This amendment requires user approval
per `feedback_no_autonomous_amendments`; it is **not** something
this dispatch can make autonomously.

### Option C (escalation needed): de-scope GF(65521)/n=4096

Drop the n=4096 cell from the GF(65521) PASS bar; keep the smaller-n
cells as is. This makes 98336ab4's downstream re-bench close on the
GF(251)/n=4096 cell only (which 74ba1cdc R1 already closed at ratio
1.466). Same escalation note as Option B.

**No autonomous amendment** is made here; this evidence is filed
for the lead's review.

## Addendum — F8 is elevated from advisory to blocking

Recorded 2026-09-08, after the round-3 dispatch, on evidence from `c7113c5a`.

The reviewer classified F8 (`receipt.rs:1315`, outlier detection pools baseline
and candidate windows under one absolute median threshold) as advisory, with the
stated reason: "Current smoke receipts report no flagged windows, so this does
not overturn their stated outcomes." That premise is false. It held only for the
protocol's own smoke receipts, whose arms are close together. The first real
family receipt to run under the protocol overturns it.

`c7113c5a`'s confirmatory receipt
(`dev/bench_results/c7113c5a/2026-09-07-c7113c5a-polynomial-confirmation`)
reports ten cells. Six carry the outcome **Unstable**, each flagging 116–119 of
its 240 windows. The worker recomputed the flagging per arm instead of over the
pool and found **zero flagged windows in every arm of every cell**, with the
widest window anywhere at 1.21x its own arm's median. The pooled count is 0 in
all four cells whose arms lie within a factor of 7 of each other, and 116–119 in
all six whose arms differ by 167x or more.

The mechanism: `receipt.rs:1252`/`:1315` pool both arms' windows, `:1382` takes
one median over the pool, and `:1543` gates the cell outcome on that fraction
ahead of the decision. When the arms differ by more than
`flagged_window_factor`, the pooled median lands inside the slower arm's spread
and about half the windows flag by construction. `abtest::flagged_windows`
documents an *execution* median, so the implementation also does not match its
own stated definition.

This is not a sensitivity nuance. It changed the reported outcome of six cells
in a committed, accepted family receipt, and a re-run is deterministic and
cannot clear it. Under the protocol's own terms an outcome that a reader cannot
recompute from the evidence is a defect in the acceptance rule, so F8 is
**blocking** and belongs with F1–F7 in the round-3 required changes.

Two consequences for the rework:

1. Flagging is computed per arm, against that arm's own median, matching the
   documented definition. A fixture must cover the large-effect-size case:
   arms differing by two orders of magnitude, each internally stable, must
   produce zero flagged windows.
2. The amendment changes a published cell outcome, which reinforces the
   direction already given on F12 — this is a material change and the protocol
   becomes version 2.

`c7113c5a` also reports a naming hazard worth carrying into the protocol text:
its baseline is gf2 and its candidate is gf2x, so `improved` means the external
arm won and `fail` means it lost. Four `fail` cells are gf2 wins. A reader of
the scorecard cannot infer the direction without the addendum, and the epic's
final scorecard aggregates families with different baselines.

### F8's blast radius is wider than the polynomial survey showed

Recorded 2026-09-08, on independent evidence from `eda07788`.

The polynomial survey's instances all sit at arm ratios of 167x and above, which
left open the reading that the pooled threshold only misbehaves at extreme

So the artifact is already visible at a 6x separation, in a different family,
with a different comparator and a different workload. The epic's remaining

changes what "unstable" means for the whole scorecard.

The fixture the rework carries for this must therefore cover the small-ratio

## Addendum — F13, a ninth blocking finding: the resolution rule

Recorded 2026-09-08, on evidence from `26465e6c`. Found after the round-3
dispatch, so the rework has not seen it.

**Location:** `dev/tools/tuning-campaign-support/src/protocol.rs:678`, the
comparison `if value - 1.0 < resolution {` inside the `worthwhile_speedup`
branch guarded by `measurement_resolution` at `:677`.

`protocol.md:191-192` states that the schema "rejects a worthwhile speedup that
lies inside that resolution (P-03)". The implementation does not enforce that
rule, in two independent ways.

**(a) The subtraction is not the declared decimal.** `1.05_f64 - 1.0` evaluates
to 0.05000000000000004441, strictly greater than the double nearest 0.05
(0.05000000000000000278). A margin exactly equal to its declared resolution
therefore passes both a float `<` and a float `<=`, and which way any given
margin falls is decided by its binary representation rather than by the rule.
Measured over margins 1.01..1.50 against an equal resolution: the subtraction
rounds up for 21 (escaping both comparisons), rounds down for 20 (caught by
`<`), and lands exact for 9 (caught only by `<=`). The 5%–10% band in which
every family in this epic declares its margins sits entirely inside the
rounds-up set, so in practice the check never fires at the boundary it exists to
guard.

**(b) The boundary belongs to the rule.** A margin exactly the size of the noise
floor is a difference the design does not resolve, so P-03 wants `<=`. As
written it is `<`, which admits a margin sitting exactly on the resolution even
under exact arithmetic.

The fix is to do the arithmetic on the declared decimal and compare with `<=` —
either on integer hundredths, or `value - 1.0 <= resolution + 1e-9`. Both were
checked across every whole percent from 1 to 50: each fires on every
exact-boundary case and on none one percent above it.

**The scope note is the larger half of this finding.** The resolution check at
`:677` is applied *only* to `worthwhile_speedup`. `equivalence_margin` (`:703`)
and `material_gap_threshold` (`:711`) receive no resolution check at all — they
are validated for finiteness, `>= 1` and a non-empty rationale, and nothing
else. Every family in this wave's surveys is a `comparator-gap` family, which
decides against `material_gap_threshold` and `equivalence_margin` and never
against `worthwhile_speedup`. So the shared tooling would not have checked a
single one of this epic's declared margins against its measurement resolution.
The defect surfaced only because one survey applied the rule to all three
margins by hand in its own harness.

A third instance of the same class was found and fixed inside that survey's own
harness: `ceil(worst * 100) / 100` inflates an exactly-whole-percent half-width
by a full percent at 0.07, 0.14 and 0.28, because `0.07 * 100` is
7.000000000000001. Whether the shared crate rounds a resolution the same way
anywhere else is an open question and is being audited.

**Required:** apply the resolution rule to all three margin fields, on the
declared decimal, with the boundary inside the rejection; fixtures for a margin
exactly equal to its resolution in each of the three fields, and for one a
percent above it.

### F13 refinements, and the contract question v2 must settle

Recorded 2026-09-08, from an independent confirmation by `26465e6c`'s owner.

The fix should converge on an idiom the crate already uses rather than
introducing one: `receipt.rs:1317` already writes
`(center - execution.ns_per_call).abs() <= 1e-9 * center.max(1.0)`. The same
shape applies at `protocol.rs:678`.

**The contract question is the part only the rework can settle.** The operator
today is `<`, so a margin exactly equal to its resolution is allowed when the
float falls that way. `protocol.md:191-192` says the schema "rejects a
worthwhile speedup that lies inside that resolution". An effect exactly the size
of the resolution is not resolvable by the design that measured it, which reads
as reject-at-or-inside, i.e. `<=`. Whichever v2 chooses, `protocol.md` must
state it and a test must pin the boundary — because **today neither operator is
what actually runs**. The independent confirmation quantifies the same defect
slightly differently from the original report (rejecting at 19 of the whole
percents 1..49 with the margin set exactly at the resolution, accepting at the
other 30, including 5, 10 and 25); the two framings agree that the outcome is
decided by representation rather than by the rule.

**Percent quantisation is a named pattern, not one instance.**
`math.ceil(x * 100) / 100` overstates any value that is exactly a whole percent
scaling upward — `0.07 * 100` is `7.000000000000001` — hitting 7, 14, 28, 55 and
56 percent. Anywhere v2 quantises a resolution or a margin to percent needs the
same treatment, so the rework should sweep for "scale by 100, then round, floor
or ceil" as a class.

### F8's onset needs dispersion as well as ratio

`26465e6c` recounted flagged windows pooled and per arm across all four of its
receipts: **zero flags either way in every cell, at arm ratios from 1.09x to
3.07x**, measured on a quiet host. That is a clean negative and it bounds the
defect from below. Together with the polynomial survey's 167x–857x instances and
the shift survey's 3-of-60 at ~6x, the picture is that a large ratio alone does
not produce the artifact — it needs a large ratio *and* real window dispersion.

The polynomial survey has since derived a closed form for the onset ratio
(`c7113c5a` commit `556e5420`). The rework's fixture should be built from that
predicate rather than from a guessed ratio, and the predicate should be checked
against all three observations above before it is trusted.

### F8 has a predicate, derived independently and validated on three cells

Recorded 2026-09-08, from `6c6b09b1`. This is what the rework's fixture should
be built on.

With equal window counts and fully separated arms, the pooled median is the
midpoint of `fast_max` and `slow_min`, so the threshold at
`flagged_window_factor = 2` becomes `fast_max + slow_min`. Windows of the slower
arm exceed it whenever

    fast_max < slow_max - slow_min

— that is, whenever the faster arm's slowest window is smaller than the slower
arm's own spread. Separation alone is not the trigger; separation measured
against the slower arm's dispersion is.

Validated on `6c6b09b1`'s three cells, reproducing each flagged count exactly:
true at a 470x ratio (19 of 60 pooled, 0 of 30 per arm on each side; gf2 windows
span 1.024x, ISA-L 1.019x, so neither arm holds a disturbed window), false at
16.8x, false at 1.7x. The worked numbers for the flagging cell: threshold
1 339 288 ns against a slow-arm minimum of 1 336 387 ns, 0.2% above it, with 19
of 30 windows above the line.

The predicate resolves what looked like an inconsistency across the wave. The
shift survey flags at ~6x while this survey's 16.8x cell does not, which no
ratio threshold explains: for arms with a 2% spread the rule bites above roughly
40x, and for a 17% spread at 6x. It also agrees with the popcount survey's clean
negative at 1.09x–3.07x.

`c7113c5a` derived a closed form for the same onset independently
(commit `556e5420`). Two independent derivations from different families are
strong evidence, and the rework should check them against each other and against
all four observations — 470x flagging, 167x–857x flagging, ~6x flagging, and
1.09x–3.07x plus 16.8x clean — before pinning a fixture. A fixture written to a
guessed ratio will pass while leaving the defect live for a different spread.

## Addendum — F14, a tenth blocking finding: the bootstrap lower endpoint

Recorded 2026-09-08 from a read-only audit of `main` at `98ac6cd1`. Found after
the round-3 dispatch. Same class as F13: a declared decimal reconstructed by
float subtraction.

**Location:** `dev/tools/tuning-campaign-support/src/abtest.rs:210-217`.

```
let alpha = 1.0 - confidence;                                        // :210
let rank = |q: f64| ((q * count as f64).ceil() as usize).clamp(1, count) - 1;  // :212
lower: replicates[rank(alpha / 2.0)],                                // :215
```

`alpha_c` never reaches this function. It arrives as `confidence`, computed at
`:292` as `1.0 - family_alpha / f64::from(comparisons)`, and `:210` reconstructs
`alpha_c` by subtracting it back out — a double round trip through `1.0` that
loses the declared decimal. At the frozen `B = 10000` and confidence 0.95:

```
1.0 - 0.95      = 0.05000000000000004441   (declared alpha_c is exactly 0.05)
alpha/2 * 10000 = 250.00000000000022737    -> ceil 251 -> index 250
0.025   * 10000 = 250.0                    -> ceil 250 -> index 249
```

The lower bound comes back as replicate 250 rather than 249 of the sorted 10000.
The upper endpoint is unaffected at 0.95.

**Scope.** The error fires exactly where `alpha_c/2 * B` lands on an integer.
Sweeping `family_alpha = 0.05` over `m = 1..32` at `B = 10000`, the lower rank is
wrong at **m = 1, 2, 5, 10 and 25** and right elsewhere; the upper rank is never
wrong in that range. All five errors are one rank too high; none goes the other
way. `m = 1` is every pilot in this epic and any confirmatory family with a
single comparison, so **every committed pilot receipt in this wave carries it**.

**Why it is blocking rather than cosmetic.** `abtest.rs:275-281` decides
`Improved` on `interval.lower >= margins.improvement` and `NotWorse` on
`interval.lower >= floor`. Both tests read the lower bound, and this defect
raises it. The bias is therefore systematically anti-conservative — marginally
more likely to declare a material gap or an equivalence than the stated rule
allows. One order statistic in 10000 is a small effect, but it is directional,
not random, and it is not the rule the protocol states.

The protocol text is not violated in form: `protocol.md:144-147` specifies
nearest-rank quantiles at `alpha_c/2` and `1 - alpha_c/2`, and `:212` implements
nearest rank correctly. The defect is entirely in what reaches it.

**Required:** stop reconstructing `alpha_c`. It is exactly `family_alpha / m`,
both declared, so pass it (or `family_alpha` and `comparisons`) into the interval
routine and derive `confidence` only for reporting. That is a signature change,
not an expression change. A local fix computing the rank on the declared
`alpha_c` removes the five cases; an epsilon on the `ceil` hides the round trip
rather than fixing it. Fixtures must cover `m = 1, 2, 5, 10, 25` at `B = 10000`.

**Consequence for evidence already published.** Every v1 interval in this wave
has a lower endpoint one rank high at `m = 1`. The v2 re-run closes it. Until
then each survey states it as a known limitation of its intervals rather than
silently carrying it, and no adoption decision rests on a v1 lower bound that
sits within one order statistic of its margin.

## Addendum — F15 and F16, from the class-partial audit

Recorded 2026-09-08 from the read-only audit of `main` at `98ac6cd1`.

### F15 (blocking) — the frozen protocol contradicts itself on the flag boundary

`protocol.md:128-129` (Outlier policy, operative prose): "A window **slower
than** `flagged_window_factor` times its cell's median is flagged and counted".
`protocol.md:209` (frozen-settings table, justification): "A window **at least
twice** the median is a disturbance, not variance of the workload."
`abtest.rs:303`: `.filter(|value| **value > factor * center)`.

Strict versus inclusive. They disagree on exactly one case — a window whose
`ns_per_call` is exactly twice the median — and the code implements the prose
while contradicting the table.

Unlike F13 and F14 there is no rounding to blame: `2.0 * center` is exact,
since multiplying by a power of two only shifts the exponent. The document
simply says two different things and the code can satisfy only one.

The case is reachable. `calls` is calibrated once per execution and held fixed
across its windows (`timing.rs:176`, loop `:178-187`), so every window of an
execution shares a denominator and `ns_per_call` is exactly proportional to
`elapsed_ns`; `WINDOWS = 5` is odd so the median is an observed sample. Windows
`[100, 100, 100, 100, 200]` give `200.0 > 200.0` — false, unflagged, though the
table calls it a disturbance by definition. Exact equality of two measured
nanosecond counts is uncommon and the finding does not claim otherwise; what
matters is the direction. The strict comparison **undercounts** disturbances,
making a cell look more stable than the protocol's own justification says it is,
and stability is what gates `Unstable` at `receipt.rs:1543`.

**Lead's direction:** the inclusive reading wins. The table's sentence is the
one that states what the setting *means* — it is the justification for the value
being 2 at all — and the operative prose should implement that meaning rather
than the reverse. It is also the conservative direction, which matters here:
F14 is anti-conservative on the lower bound and this is anti-conservative on
stability, and two independent thumbs on the same side of the scale is not a
posture this epic can defend. So `>=` in the code, "at least" in the prose at
`:128-129`, table unchanged, and a fixture pinning the exactly-equal window.

This interacts with F8: fixing the pooling without settling this boundary leaves
the flag count ambiguous at its own threshold. Settle both in the same change.

For contrast, the neighbouring rule is clean and needs no work.
`max_flagged_fraction`: prose `:130-131` "exceeds", table `:210` "More than a
tenth", `receipt.rs:1543` `flagged_fraction > max_flagged_fraction` — all three
strict, and `flagged as f64 / total as f64` is a correctly rounded division, so
24/240 and 6/60 both produce the double nearest 0.1 and compare equal rather
than above.

### F16 (blocking) — the guard that should keep document and code in step is blind

`tests/protocol_contracts.rs:1708`,
`protocol_document_pins_the_frozen_shared_settings`, is cited at
`protocol.md:196-198` as what "keeps this table equal to the code". It does not.

**(a)** It parses each table row and keeps only `columns[1]` (name) and
`columns[2]` (value), then asserts name-to-value equality against
`SHARED_SETTINGS.table()` (`:1712-1722`). Column 3 — the justification, where
every one of these settings states its *semantics* rather than its magnitude,
and where "at least twice the median" lives — is parsed and discarded. F15 is
therefore structurally invisible to CI: the table could read "at least twice",
"more than twice" or "twice or thereabouts" and the test passes identically so
long as the number stays 2. Every semantic divergence of that shape is
undetectable.

**(b)** `for rule in 1..=22` at `:1725`, while the protocol declares 23 rules:
`grep -c "^| P-" protocol.md` returns 23, and P-23 has its own row at `:380` and
four cross-references at `:39`, `:40`, `:51` and `:306`. P-23's presence is
never asserted. The impact is small — the loop only checks that the string
appears — but the shape is F13's worse half exactly: a rule stated for a class,
enforced over part of the class, this time in the test layer.

**Required:** the pinning test asserts the justification column too, so a
semantic change to the table fails CI; and it derives its rule range from the
document rather than hard-coding an upper bound, so adding P-24 cannot silently
escape it.

F16 is why F15, and arguably F8 and F13, survived two independent methodology
reviews and a passing CI: the mechanism that was supposed to catch
document-versus-code drift was checking magnitudes and ignoring meanings.

## Addendum — audit closure: F14 amplified, an ordering constraint, and F17

Recorded 2026-09-08 from the completed read-only audit of `main` at `98ac6cd1`.

### F14 is worse than recorded: the independent check shares the defect

P-20 (`protocol.md:377`) is the rule that makes a wrong interval visible: "The
bootstrap interval and decision recompute from the raw pairs at the family
confidence; a contradicting runner claim rejects the cell." It cannot see F14,
because producer and checker run the same expression. The runner calls
`bonferroni_confidence` then `paired_bootstrap_speedup` at
`benchmark-ab-runner.rs:628-630`; the acceptance checker makes the identical
pair of calls at `receipt.rs:1021` and `:1482`. Both land in `abtest.rs:210`,
both take rank 250 instead of 249, both agree, and P-20 reports no
contradiction.

So every committed receipt at family confidence 0.95 carries a lower bound one
order statistic high **and has been certified correct by the tool**. This is not
a lax checker; it is a checker sharing the defect it should catch.

Consequence the rework must plan for: fixing `abtest.rs` changes the lower bound
of every existing receipt, so any receipt re-accepted afterwards will disagree
with its recorded interval. That is a **re-run trigger**, not a bug in the fix,
and it should be stated in the v2 amendment record rather than discovered during
re-acceptance.

### Ordering constraint: F15 must be settled before F8's fixture is written

The two are not merely interacting; the F8 onset predicate is *defined* by the
F15 comparison. Verified by reimplementing `median` and `flagged_windows`
byte-for-byte from `abtest.rs:138-152` and `:298-305` and running 20 000
randomized separated two-arm cases at 120 windows per arm: the predicate
`fast_max < slow_max - slow_min` agreed with the code's actual flagging 20 000
times and disagreed 0 times.

A constructed exact instance shows the coupling. Fast arm `fast_max = 4`; slow
arm `slow_min = 10`, `slow_max = 14`, so `slow_max = fast_max + slow_min`
exactly. Pooled median 7, `2 * centre` = 14, `slow_max` = 14. The code as
written (`>`) flags 0 of 240; the inclusive reading (`>=`) flags 1 of 240. So
settling F15 to `>=` moves the onset to `fast_max <= slow_max - slow_min`.

**A fixture written for F8 before F15 is settled pins the wrong predicate.**
Settle F15 first, then write the per-arm fixtures against the corrected form.

One qualification belongs in the record with the predicate: it is the onset law
for the **separated** regime only. On fully overlapping arms the same 20 000-case
test agreed 12 957 times and disagreed 7 043. Separation is the regime the
pooling defect bites in, and a large arm ratio guarantees it, but the closed form
should be stated with "under separation" attached rather than as a general law.

## Addendum — F18, the finding that finishes F13

Recorded 2026-09-08. **Blocking.** The declared `measurement_resolution` is
never checked against the pilot receipt pinned as having measured it. The
content pin proves *which* artifact; nothing proves the number came out of it.

`protocol.md:226-229` states the property: "pilot-measured measurement
resolution with the content-pinned pilot receipt path and digest that observed
it". `protocol.md:91-92` states the enforcement: "the acceptance tool verifies
the snapshot's digest and `pilot` label and rejects missing, self-referential or
non-pilot evidence (P-03)". `receipt.rs:1623-1686`,
`verify_resolution_evidence`, implements that enforcement sentence faithfully —
and the enforcement sentence is a *projection* of the property.

Everything the sentence enumerates is present, which is worth stating because
this function is otherwise a model of the thing done right: digest via
`pin.verify_content` (`:1662`), pilot label (`:1674`), missing handled
structurally at `protocol.rs:690`, non-pilot at `:1676`, self-reference at
`:1650` checking both path and digest against the receipt under evaluation, and
a path-traversal guard at `:1637-1648` that F17 shows is absent on the
neighbouring pin.

What is missing is everything the sentence does not mention:

**(a)** The declared resolution is never compared with anything the pinned pilot
observed. The pilot is decoded — `BenchmarkReceipt::decode(&bytes)` at `:1673` —
and then only `label` is read. The bytes needed to recompute the widest relative
half-width are in hand and discarded. `half_width` appears nowhere in the crate.

**(b)** The pilot's family is never compared with the confirming addendum's.
`BenchmarkReceipt` carries `issue` (`:287`), `campaign_id` (`:286`) and its own
`addendum` pin (`:300`), so family identity is available in the verified bytes.
Family X's confirmation may pin a pilot from family Y, or from another issue, and
P-03 accepts it.

**The exploitable direction is anti-conservative.** The resolution is used as a
gate only at `protocol.rs:677-683`, where a `worthwhile_speedup` must lie outside
it, so a *smaller* declared resolution makes that gate easier to pass. An
addendum may declare `measurement_resolution: 0.001` while pinning a pilot that
observed 0.04, declare a worthwhile speedup of 1.01, and pass: the pilot is
present, digest-verified, labelled pilot, not self-referential, and its actual
numbers are never read. Concrete instance from this wave: the popcount pilot at
`dev/bench_results/26465e6c/2026-09-08-26465e6c-popcount-pilot` observed a widest
relative half-width of 0.0401, and its addendum could have declared any
resolution at all without acceptance noticing.

**Composed with F13, the resolution gate is ornamental end to end.** Three links,
all broken: the resolution is not checked against its evidence (F18); two of the
three margins are never checked against the resolution at all (F13, scope half);
the third is checked with a comparison that fails at the boundary (F13, float
half). No declared margin in this epic has been checked against a resolution, and
no declared resolution has been checked against a pilot.

**Required.** Recompute the resolution from the pinned pilot bytes inside
`verify_resolution_evidence`, using the derivation the addendum used — widest
relative confidence-interval half-width over the pilot's cells, rounded up — and
reject a declared resolution below it. Rounding up makes the check
`declared + tolerance >= recomputed`, so a family may declare a *more*
conservative resolution than its pilot measured and never a less conservative
one. Do the rounding on the decimal, not via `ceil(x * 100.0)`, or the fix
reintroduces F13's arithmetic. Add the family-identity comparison in the same
function while the pilot is decoded; both are cheap, since the bytes are already
verified and in scope at `:1662`.

### Immediate consequence for this wave, independent of the rework

declared `measurement_resolution` is at least the widest relative

the two numbers. A declared resolution below its pilot's observation invalidates
every margin decided against it, and the tooling will not say so.

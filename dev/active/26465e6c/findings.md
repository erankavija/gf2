# Population-count and fused-reduction baselines

## Question

Does gf2's production population-count dispatcher
(`crates/gf2-core/src/kernels/ops.rs::popcount`) hold up against open-source
population-count implementations and against gf2's own internal alternatives on the prepared
Ryzen 9 5900X, across the input sizes, alignments, and bit patterns the [Zen 3 measurement
contract](../1a379447-zen3-cpu-performance/measurement-contract.md) requires? Separately, does
gf2's fused AND+population-count kernel
(`crates/gf2-kernels-simd/src/x86/avx2.rs::avx2_and_popcnt`) -- which is not reachable through
any public `gf2-core` dispatcher today -- offer a material gap over the two-pass
`and_inplace`-then-`popcount` pattern a consumer reaches through the public API?

This issue establishes reproducible baselines only. It changes no production code under
`crates/`.

## Methodology

Every receipt in this issue follows the frozen [Zen 3 benchmark
protocol](../f547c394/protocol.md) version 1 and its [addendum
schema](../f547c394/addendum.schema.json), accepted by `benchmark-acceptance` from
`dev/tools/tuning-campaign-support`. Two families are frozen, each with a separate exploratory
pilot addendum (measurement-resolution evidence) and confirmatory addendum:

- `popcount-baselines` -- [pilot addendum](addendum-popcount-pilot.json),
  [confirmatory addendum](addendum-popcount.json).
- `and-popcnt-baselines` -- [pilot addendum](addendum-and-popcnt-pilot.json),
  [confirmatory addendum](addendum-and-popcnt.json).

Every cell's objective is `comparator-gap`: gf2's own approach is the baseline arm in every
cell, and the question each cell answers is whether the candidate arm achieves a material
throughput gap against it at the family confidence level, per the protocol's decision rule.
Both families declare a measurement resolution derived from their own pilot, a material-gap
threshold of 1.10 and an equivalence margin outside that resolution; the derivation is
performed by [`survey/freeze-confirmatory.py`](survey/freeze-confirmatory.py) from the pilot
receipt the addendum pins, never by hand.

**Status of the confirmations.** Protocol version 2 is in flight, and confirmation under a new
protocol version starts from fresh samples. The `popcount-baselines` confirmatory receipt
below was taken and accepted under version 1 and is committed as version-1 data, which an
amendment retains; it is **not final** and is re-run under version 2 before any conclusion
here is settled. The `and-popcnt-baselines` confirmation is **not yet run** and is deliberately
held for version 2. Both families' pilots and every version-independent result -- the pins, the
controls, the equivalence validation and the emitted-instruction evidence -- stand as they are.
All four campaigns run through one launcher,
[`survey/run-campaign.sh`](survey/run-campaign.sh), which derives each cell's workload from
its addendum and refuses to launch a plan whose cells disagree with the addendum's.

The three `native`, `tuned-portable` and `conservative-portable` gf2 arms come from one
binary, built with no `RUSTFLAGS` and no `target-cpu`, and no single arm mixes two regimes.
The build identity names the arm's **instruction-selection regime**, which is what the
protocol's build vocabulary distinguishes and what
[`survey/objdump-evidence.txt`](survey/objdump-evidence.txt) verifies for each arm separately:

- `native` reaches AVX2 through runtime detection, exactly as a consumer of the shipped
  library does;
- `tuned-portable` opts one function into `popcnt` through `#[target_feature]`, which the
  disassembly shows as the `popcnt` instruction inside an otherwise baseline binary;
- `conservative-portable` opts into nothing, which the disassembly shows as the portable SWAR
  sequence (`movdqa`/`pand`/`psrlw`/`psadbw`, no `popcnt`, no AVX2).

Building gf2 with `-C target-cpu=native` would measure a different artifact from the one gf2
ships, because the production dispatcher's whole design is runtime selection on a portable
build. The external arms are built `-O3 -march=native`, which is how their upstreams are meant
to be built; libpopcnt additionally dispatches on CPUID at run time.

| Mula's carry-save network engages at 512-byte blocks | `and $0xfffffffffffffff0,%rdx` masking the trip count to a multiple of 16 vectors and `add $0x200,%rax` advancing the pointer one block, matching `limit = size - size % 16` in the vendored source |

The cells bracket the two observed thresholds rather than assuming them: 4 words (below gf2's
8-word selection) and 8 words (at it); 63 words (below Mula's 64-word carry-save engagement)
and 64 words (at it).

### Validated equivalence

[`survey/cross-check.sh`](survey/cross-check.sh) is the committed evidence for REQ-03; its
output is [`survey/cross-check.log`](survey/cross-check.log). It fails closed on three checks:

1. **Identical buffers at identical alignment.** Every arm binary prints, under `--alignment`,
   the byte alignment of the fixture window each `word_offset` selects; the three outputs must
   be identical. Both sides allocate a 32-byte aligned base of `words + 4` words and hand out
   the window at `word_offset`, so offset 0 is vector-aligned and 1, 2 and 3 sit 8, 16 and 24
   bytes off it.
2. **Independent reference.** Each external arm re-derives every count with
   `__builtin_popcountll` over its own buffer under `--selftest`, across word counts
   0/1/2/7/8/9/63/64/65/256/4096, three bit patterns and every supported word offset.
3. **Identical returned counts.** For each tuple of (words, seed, pattern, word_offset), every
   applicable arm's `--check` mode returns the identical count. The tuples cover one per
   confirmatory cell plus the word-boundary sizes 0, 1, 63, 64 and 65 at all four word offsets,
   for both the population-count and the fused-reduction arms.

Bit-length tail semantics are covered by `survey/gf2-side/tests/correctness.rs`, which drives
`BitVec::count_ones` -- the public tail-aware path that delegates to
`kernels::ops::popcount` -- at bit lengths 0, 1, 63, 64, 65, 127, 128, 4095 and 4096 and
compares against an independently re-derived count, and which also asserts the fixture
alignment invariant and that the whole-consumer op returns the kernel-isolated count.

Consumer-equivalent timing: the two-pass arm allocates its temporary buffer, runs
`and_inplace` over it and then runs `popcount` over it, all inside the timed body. Nothing is
hoisted out of the measurement and no traffic the consumer pays is omitted. The whole-consumer
cell additionally reports the protocol's conversion costs, measured in the same process after
the timed windows: `setup_ns` for fixture construction, `pack_ns` for the per-call temporary
buffer the two-pass model materializes, and `dispatch_ns` for the per-call runtime backend
resolution each arm performs. Those probes quantify the costs; they never subtract them.

### Why the fused-reduction family has no external candidate

Neither libpopcnt nor Mula's sse-popcount exposes a fused AND-then-popcount (or XOR-then-
popcount) operation; both offer only a single-buffer population count. This is reported as an
unmatched result per the measurement contract rather than substituted with a different
operation: the `and-popcnt-baselines` family compares gf2's fused kernel only against its own
internal alternatives (a scalar single-pass control and the two-pass public-API pattern).

### Why the Mula reference is unavailable at unaligned offsets

The vendored `popcnt_AVX2_harley_seal` takes its input through
`AVX2_harley_seal::popcnt(const __m256i*, ...)`, which the compiler lowers to aligned 256-bit
loads: 47 `vmovdqa` and no `vmovdqu`. Reading through it on an unaligned pointer is undefined
behavior and was observed to fault on this host. The arm therefore fails closed on
`word_offset != 0` and the alignment cell carries no Mula candidate. This is a property of the
reference implementation, recorded rather than worked around; the survey does not modify the
vendored upstream file.

### Why no 6- or 12-physical-core or 24-logical-CPU arm

Population count and fused AND+popcount are leaf, kernel-isolated operations always invoked
single-threaded inside a larger consumer routine; this family's research question -- which
implementation is fastest at a given input size and cache state -- is a single-core property.
A wider core arm without genuine multi-threaded work inside the arm process would only vary
CPU affinity, not test throughput scaling, and would not be a meaningful measurement per the
Zen 3 measurement contract's "where meaningful" allowance. Multicore scaling of a popcount- or
AND-popcount-shaped workload is a property of the *consumer* that parallelizes many such
calls (for example a decoder's per-row syndrome weight), which belongs to that consumer's own
family, not to this leaf-kernel survey.

## Findings

### The issue's premise is falsified: external implementations do beat gf2's dispatcher

The background to this issue treats published results from other processors as hypotheses to be
tested. Tested on this host, four of the nine confirmatory cells find a material gap **against**
gf2, at a family-wise alpha of 0.05 over nine comparisons:

- libpopcnt is 1.4775x faster on an all-ones 64-word buffer and 1.3274x faster on a
  cache-resident 16384-word buffer;
- the Mula AVX2 Harley-Seal reference is 1.2019x faster on an all-zero 64-word buffer and
  1.2015x faster on a random 64-word buffer;
- gf2's own AVX2 nibble-LUT kernel, called directly, is 1.5738x faster than gf2's production
  dispatcher on a 4-word buffer.

Every one of these intervals excludes the 1.10 material-gap threshold from above. gf2's
population-count dispatcher is not the fastest implementation available on this processor at
these sizes, and this survey records that rather than reporting the arms that lose.

### gf2's scalar/vector selection threshold is too high

The dispatcher selects the scalar path at or below 7 words (`cmp $0x7,%rsi`). At 4 words the
AVX2 nibble-LUT kernel called directly is 1.5738x faster, interval [1.5700, 1.5826]. The
supporting dispatch probe ([`survey/dispatch-probe.log`](survey/dispatch-probe.log)) shows the
gap is the kernels, not the dispatch: every arm's per-call runtime backend resolution measures
0 ns, because at `lto = true` and `codegen-units = 1` the compiler folds
`gf2_kernels_simd::detect()` to a constant function-pointer load. The same probe shows the two
arms converging from 8 words upward (3 ns and 3 ns at 8 words, 33 ns and 32 ns at 256), which
is what the dispatcher selecting the same kernel above its threshold should look like.

At the threshold itself, the forced scalar `POPCNT` loop is 1.0882x faster than the dispatcher,
interval [1.0839, 1.0932]. That interval sits below the 1.10 threshold and above 1/1.06, so the
cell is `not-material`: at 8 words the choice between the vector kernel and a `POPCNT` loop does
not matter, and the interesting boundary is below it, not at it.

**This is a survey, so nothing here changes the threshold.** Moving `SIMD_MIN_WORDS` is a
production change requiring its own before/after evidence under the measurement contract, and
it belongs to a separate issue.

### The carry-save crossover is measured where the disassembly says it is

Mula's Harley-Seal network processes 16 vectors per iteration and so engages only from 512
bytes, which the disassembly shows as `and $0xfffffffffffffff0,%rdx` masking the trip count and
`add $0x200,%rax` advancing the pointer. Bracketing that boundary rather than assuming it:

| Words | Bytes | CSA network | Mula vs gf2 | Interval | Outcome |
|---:|---:|---|---:|---|---|
| 63 | 504 | never engages | 0.7766 | [0.7721, 0.7822] | `fail` -- gf2 is 29% faster |
| 64 | 512 | engages once | 1.2015 | [1.1947, 1.2074] | `pass` -- Mula is 20% faster |

One word of input flips a 29% deficit into a 20% advantage. This is the observed threshold on
this processor, not an imported one, and it is the concrete answer to REQ-04's requirement to
observe the emitted instructions instead of importing another processor's dispatch thresholds.

### The internal controls behave as controls should

The portable `count_ones` control is 2.9x slower than the dispatcher on a 256-word buffer
(0.3479, interval [0.3464, 0.3516], `regressed`/`fail`), and 3.2x slower at 524288 words in the
supporting probe. That is the expected cost of the SWAR lowering the disassembly shows, and it
is preserved as a losing arm rather than dropped. The forced `POPCNT` control is competitive at
the vector boundary and 1.3x slower than the dispatcher at 524288 words in the probe, which is
the AVX2 kernel's throughput advantage appearing as the buffer grows.

### The fused AND+popcount kernel wins against every alternative available to a consumer

The pilot measures gf2's `avx2_and_popcnt` against both internal alternatives, and every
candidate loses to it:

- the single-pass scalar control reaches 0.3252 of the fused kernel's throughput on a
  cache-resident 4096-word buffer and 0.6533 on a streaming 524288-word buffer;
- the two-pass `and_inplace`-then-`popcount` pattern -- the only path a consumer can reach
  through `gf2-core`'s public API today -- reaches 0.3869 in the whole-consumer cell, with the
  temporary buffer allocation and the extra pass inside the timed body.

The whole-consumer cell's conversion record quantifies why. At 4096 words the fused kernel
takes a median 577 ns per call and the two-pass model 1492 ns, and the two-pass arm's reported
`pack_ns` -- the temporary buffer it allocates and copies inside the timed body -- is 482 ns of
that. The buffer alone costs 84% of the fused kernel's entire call and accounts for a little
over half the 915 ns difference between the two arms; the remainder is the second pass over
the temporary. Both arms report a `dispatch_ns` of 0 and 1 ns respectively, so none of the gap
is dispatch.

These are exploratory figures. The confirmation that would let them support a decision is held
for protocol version 2. What they already establish is a direction so large -- a factor of about
2.6 -- that it is not a candidate for being an artifact of the measurement resolution of 0.03
this family measured.

### Two harness defects found and fixed in this issue

Both were in the survey harness this issue owns, both would have silently invalidated
comparisons, and both are now regression-tested.

1. **The gf2 arm's fixture was not the fixture the external arms timed.** `make_words` built the
   padded base array and then returned `result[word_offset..][..words].to_vec()`, a fresh
   allocation at whatever alignment the allocator chose. The C arms allocate their base through
   `posix_memalign(32)` and hand out the window at `word_offset`, so their offsets 1, 2 and 3
   sit 8, 16 and 24 bytes off a vector boundary. The alignment cell would therefore have
   compared an aligned external buffer against a gf2 buffer at an unrelated alignment, while
   claiming to vary alignment. Every arm now prints its window alignment under `--alignment`
   and `cross-check.sh` diffs the three outputs.
2. **The gf2 arm rotated eight fixture banks in every cell.** The canonical arm
   (`ab-smoke-workload.rs`) and the C arms allocate `FIXTURE_BANKS` banks only for a streaming
   cell and one otherwise. The gf2 arm rotated all eight unconditionally, so in the cold and
   warm cells it worked over an eight-times larger working set than the arm it was compared
   against -- 1 MiB against 128 KiB in the cache-resident cell, which straddles this
   processor's 512 KiB L2.

Both defects were present in the receipt taken on 2026-09-07. That receipt is retained at
`dev/bench_results/26465e6c/2026-09-07-26465e6c-popcount-pilot` and still passes acceptance,
because acceptance reads only receipt-local snapshot bytes; it is superseded as resolution
evidence and cited by nothing. Its exact inputs remain in its own `plan.json`.

It is also the only receipt here whose recorded arm paths name a `git worktree` checkout. The
runner records the absolute path of each executable it launches, so the later campaigns stage
their arm binaries outside the checkout first and record a path that outlives the worktree;
acceptance identifies an arm by `executable_sha256` either way, and every receipt's arm digests
are checked against `survey/arm-provenance.json`. The 2026-09-07 receipt predates that and is
left byte-for-byte as taken.

### A protocol defect this issue hit in its own derivation

The protocol requires a declared effect to lie outside the pilot-measured resolution. Deciding
that comparison in binary floating point does not work: `1.05 - 1.0` is `0.05000000000000004`,
so an equivalence margin of 1.05 against a measured resolution of 0.05 -- exactly this family's
case -- passes a strict comparison by accident and an equivalence claim ends up resting on the
noise floor. This issue's own derivation had that bug and now decides the boundary on the
integer percent, with a selftest pinning margins on, just inside and just outside the
resolution.

The same class of defect is live in the shared tooling at
`dev/tools/tuning-campaign-support/src/protocol.rs:678`, which guards `worthwhile_speedup`
with `value - 1.0 < resolution`. For a worthwhile speedup declared exactly at the resolution,
that check rejects at 19 of the whole percents in 1..49 and accepts at the other 30, decided by
the representation rather than by the rule. It does not affect this issue -- both families are
`comparator-gap`, so `worthwhile_speedup` is null -- and it is reported to the protocol rework
rather than fixed here, because that crate is under concurrent rework.

A second instance of the same class was found while fixing the first: `math.ceil(x * 100) / 100`
overstates a value whenever `x` is exactly a whole percent that scales upward, since
`0.07 * 100` is `7.000000000000001`. Five whole percents in 1..99 are affected: 7, 14, 28, 55
and 56. The resolution ceiling now runs on the shortest round-tripping decimal.

### What the cold cells actually establish

The methodology review of `f547c394` found that `timing.rs:176` calibrates by running the
workload before the first timed window, so a cell declaring `cache_state: cold` has had its
working set touched before the first sample. Six of the nine confirmatory popcount cells
declare `cold`.

What those cells establish as measured, and what they do not:

- **They are valid paired comparisons.** Both arms of a cell run the identical calibration
  through the identical timing path, so whatever cache state calibration leaves behind, it
  leaves behind for both arms. The speedup of medians and its interval are unaffected: this is
  a systematic condition shared by the pair, not a bias toward one arm.
- **They are not cold-cache measurements.** A cell declaring `cold` is measuring a
  warmed-through working set. For the small cells this distinction is close to empty -- a 4-,
  8-, 63- or 64-word buffer is 32 to 512 bytes and is L1-resident after a single touch no
  matter what the protocol declares, so `cold` was never achievable at those sizes by any
  calibration policy. For the 256-word alignment cell the same holds at 2 KiB.
- **The distinction that would bite is at the large sizes, and no cell relies on it.** The two
  cells where the working set exceeds cache are declared `warm` (16384 words) and `streaming`
  (524288 words), and `streaming` is unaffected because it rotates banks by design.

My view for the version-2 rework, since the fixture work put the timing path in front of me:
**the cold cells here are salvageable, and relabeling is the honest repair rather than
re-measurement.** At every size this family declares `cold`, the buffer is small enough that no
calibration policy could deliver a genuinely cold cache; the declaration was aspirational from
the start. What version 2 should require is either a `cold` policy that measures a single
untimed-calibration first window, or -- better for kernels this small -- that a family not
declare `cold` for working sets that fit L1, since the label then promises something the
hardware will not deliver. The one change that would materially alter a number here is a
`cold` policy that calibrates on a separate buffer, and that would only affect a size range
where this family has no cell.

### A note on the benchmark mutex

The CCX1 mutex starved every exclusive benchmark runner for the first hour of this session:
`cargo-budget.sh` took the shared side with `flock -s -n`, and because a pending exclusive
waiter does not block a new shared acquisition on Linux, a steady stream of sibling test runs
never left a zero-holder instant. Four benchmark runners waited up to 39 minutes. This is fixed
outside this issue by a turnstile both sides pass through.

Observation, not work: the shared side should not additionally be bounded per acquirer. The
failure was an ordering property, not a volume one, and a turnstile fixes ordering directly. A
per-acquirer bound adds a second policy that can starve builds instead, and it needs tuning
against a workload that changes every wave.

## Recommendations

1. **Do not adopt an external dependency on this evidence.** Both external arms win in a
   specific size band and lose outside it: Mula loses by 29% at 63 words and wins by 20% at 64,
   and neither library offers the fused AND-popcount that is gf2's largest measured advantage.
   The maintenance and dependency cost of vendoring either is not repaid by a win confined to a
   band a caller does not control.
2. **Open a separate issue for the scalar/vector selection threshold.** The 1.5738x gap at 4
   words is gf2 losing to gf2, needs no external dependency, and is a production change with its
   own before/after evidence requirement.
3. **Open a separate issue for exposing `avx2_and_popcnt` through a public dispatcher.** It beats
   the only path a consumer can reach today by a factor of about 2.6 including the temporary
   buffer, and it is unreachable outside `gf2_kernels_simd::detect()`.
4. **Re-run both confirmations under protocol version 2** before any of the above is acted on.

## Open questions

1. Where between 4 and 8 words does gf2's scalar path stop losing to the vector kernel? This
   survey brackets the declared threshold but does not locate the true crossover, which is what
   a threshold change would need.
2. Does the carry-save crossover at 64 words hold for buffers that are not multiples of the
   512-byte block? Every cell here sits at a clean multiple or one word below.
3. Would a gf2 kernel that engages a carry-save network above 64 words recover the 20% Mula
   takes at that size? This survey measures the gap; it does not attribute it to the CSA network
   as against any other difference between the two implementations.

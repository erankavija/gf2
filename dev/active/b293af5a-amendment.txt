## Background

`8e4e19a0` measured TVD(perm, U) vs TVD(det, U) over F_q, q∈{3,5,7}. Computing a permanent is O(n·2^n), so the CPU harness could only afford N = 500/200/50 samples at q=3 n = 24/28/32. The Monte-Carlo TVD estimator has a noise floor ≈ √((q−1)/(2πN)); at N=50 that is ≈0.1, far above the true (tiny) TVD_perm at large n. The measured TVD_perm at q=3 n=28/32 (0.0417, 0.133) was therefore dominated by sampling noise, *masking* the convergence — those three cells were excluded from the criterion-6 verdict via a user-approved amendment (`8e4e19a0` Amendments §3). F_5/F_7 were capped at n≤14 for the same cost reason. The convergence is the reference paper's (arXiv 2407.20205) headline observation and HKS Theorem 1.2's subject, so reproducing it robustly is publication-relevant.

The epic's GPU batch-permanent path (`gf2_algebra::gpu::permanent_batch_bipedal{3,5,7}`, issues `2fbbdfa5`/`9480f8a6`/`ad55b777`/`5c0505b2`) runs ~28–30× CPU-SIMD throughput at n=24–28 and is *embarrassingly parallel over independent matrices* (each uniformity sample is an independent matrix). It can lift N by 2–3 orders of magnitude in the same wall-clock, dropping the noise floor below the true TVD_perm so the convergence is observed rather than noise-masked.

## Scope

GPU-accelerated re-sample of the `8e4e19a0` uniformity experiment, reusing the existing GPU batch permanent + `gf2_core::field::det`, focused on the cells where `8e4e19a0` was noise-limited: q=3 large n (n∈{24,28,32}, previously excluded) and q=5/q=7 extended beyond n≤14.

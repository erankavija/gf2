## Operations and cost boundaries

The campaign uses four distinct questions. They never share a timing cell.

| Question | Baseline operation | Compared operation | Cost boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| `isolated-fused-parity` | One call to the detected bundle's `and_popcnt_fn` over one $W$-word row and the $W$-word vector, reached through the bundle function pointer behind an optimisation barrier | The same entry with the single candidate fusion kernel body | The fused AND and population count only. Fixture generation, row selection, the parity fold, output allocation, and result observation stay outside timing. Isolated evidence is attribution; it never authorises adoption. |
| `allocated-matvec` | One public `BitMatrix::matvec(&x)` returning a fresh `BitVec` | The same public route with the candidate kernel in the detected bundle | Route selection, the per-row bundle call, the parity fold, the output `BitVec` allocation, and every output append are inside timing. Matrix and vector construction, validation, and disposal of the returned object stay outside. Reported in nanoseconds per public `matvec`. |
| `matvec-vs-m4ri` | One public `BitMatrix::matvec(&x)` on a fresh gf2 matrix and vector, with the returned `BitVec` allocated and released inside the window | M4RI `mzd_init` for `A`, `x` and `y`, bit-wise packing, `mzd_mul(y, A, x, 0)`, `mzd_read_bit` unpacking to a gf2 `BitVec`, and disposal of every `mzd_t` | Both arms charge construction, conversion, execution, and output. Fixture generation and validation stay outside timing. Not an adoption objective. |
| `scalar-reference` | One public `BitMatrix::matvec(&x)` from an executable built without the `simd` feature, which reaches the four-accumulator scalar row-parity route | The same public call in the ordinary `simd` build | The `allocated-matvec` boundary exactly. Reference and attribution only; this addendum proposes no API and names no scalar production candidate. |

the compiler-provenance regression
[`survey/check-m4ri-build-provenance.sh`](survey/check-m4ri-build-provenance.sh).
The external arm is M4RI [AlbrechtBard2026] release `20260122` under
`GPL-2.0-or-later`, identified by the archive, installed-library, probe
executable, and build-record digests of that record. Its six qualified shapes
are `1x1`, `63x63`, `64x64`, `65x65`, `65x512` and `65x4096`, and the matched

### M4RI charged components

The matched-operation specification enumerates six components of the external
whole-consumer cost. Each comparator cell charges them as follows, and the gf2
arm of the same cell charges the analogous component on its own side:

| Component of the specification | Fresh whole-consumer cell | Retained-state cell | gf2 counterpart |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. gf2 input construction or retained input state | outside timing | outside timing | matrix and vector construction, outside timing |
| 2. `mzd_init` for `A`, `x`, `y` with zero initialization | inside timing | `y` only, inside timing | none; the gf2 input is already canonical |
| 3. bit-wise packing into public M4RI coordinates | inside timing | outside timing, retained | none; the gf2 input is already canonical |
| 4. `mzd_mul(y, A, x, 0)` execution | inside timing | inside timing | the public `matvec` call, inside timing |
| 5. `mzd_read_bit` unpacking to a gf2 `BitVec` | inside timing | inside timing | the output allocation and its appends, inside timing |
| 6. disposal of every owned `mzd_t` | inside timing | `y` only, inside timing | disposal of the returned `BitVec`, inside timing |

Both arms of every comparator cell therefore enter their timed window holding
the same canonical gf2 words and leave it having produced the same row
parities, having released everything they allocated, and both declare
conversion and setup costs included. A cell that charged packing on one side
and not the other would compare two different operations, which is the
substitution the specification forbids.

The comparator family's gf2 arm charges one component the `allocated-matvec`
family's gf2 arm does not: releasing the returned `BitVec`, which the allocated
family keeps for validation outside its window. The two families' per-call
numbers are therefore not interchangeable, and no cell of one family is a
baseline, a pilot, or a resolution source for the other.

## Selected production route

The primary route is the public `BitMatrix::matvec`. Its selector compares the
row stride against the conservative eight-word threshold and takes the SIMD
lane at and above it, so every anchor stride of this addendum is on the SIMD
lane. That lane calls the bundle's fused AND-population-count entry once per
row without a temporary buffer or a second pass, and the committed release
assembly
[`crates/gf2-core/src/matrix.asm.txt`](../../../crates/gf2-core/src/matrix.asm.txt)
identifies the call site as an indirect call through the bundle and the
dispatched symbol as `gf2_kernels_simd::x86::avx2::fns::and_popcnt_fn`, whose
body is the AVX2 nibble-lookup loop `avx2_and_popcnt` (source evidence, claims
on `crates/gf2-kernels-simd/src/x86/avx2.rs`).

A consumer therefore reaches the kernel through a function pointer no caller
sees through. An arm compiled in one link-time-optimised unit with the bundle
does see through it and measures a devirtualised copy instead of the production
route; the count and fused-reduction survey records that exact failure and its
remedy (`dev/active/5cbb6545/findings.md` § Arm fidelity). Every arm of this
addendum places the same optimisation barrier around the bundle, and each arm
reports the route it observed for its own width.

The anchor strides are $W \in \{8,9,63,64,65\}$ words. A `BitMatrix` derives
its stride as $\lceil \text{cols}/64 \rceil$, so an anchor's column count is
$64W$ and its logical tail is empty:

| Cell suffix | $W$ | Columns | Rows | Matrix bytes | Output bits |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `8w` | 8 | 512 | 1024 | 65,536 | 1024 |
| `9w` | 9 | 576 | 1024 | 73,728 | 1024 |
| `63w` | 63 | 4032 | 1024 | 516,096 | 1024 |
| `64w` | 64 | 4096 | 1024 | 524,288 | 1024 |
| `65w` | 65 | 4160 | 1024 | 532,480 | 1024 |

Rows are fixed at 1024 for every gf2-only allocated cell so that the output
allocation and its 1024 appends cost the same in each, leaving the stride the
only quantity that varies across the family. The 1024-row, 4096-column anchor
is the shape the predecessor profile already attributes, where the fused kernel
holds most of the sampled allocated consumer, the entry and the SIMD lane hold
the rest, one output allocation and its bit-append are the conversion cost, and
the dispatch probe reads below one nanosecond per call
(`dev/active/04b85d10/findings.md` § Attribution, row *Dense matvec
1024x4096*). That attribution is at 64-word rows only. At eight-word rows the
per-row entry, the parity fold, and the output append hold a larger share of
the same call, so this addendum assumes no fixed kernel share and lets the
allocated family, not the isolated family, decide adoption.

The harness verifies every stated stride, column count, row count, and route
from the constructed public objects before enabling timing. A mismatch makes
the cell unavailable; it never substitutes another shape.

## Input identities and correctness

The campaign seed is `0x2037_941f_96c9_4b81`. Cells are ordered first by the
question order in this document, then by the order of each listed axis. Cell
ordinal zero uses the campaign seed; ordinal $i$ uses the $i$-th subsequent
SplitMix64 output as its workload seed. Each additional fixture bank consumes
the next SplitMix64 output from that cell's stream. The implementation is the
canonical `tuning_campaign_support::abtest::SplitMix64`; substituting another
generator or hashing cell-name text is not permitted.

SplitMix64 fills the matrix words in canonical row-major order and then the
vector words. Every generated word is one full 64-bit output, so each stored
bit is an independent fair coin and the expected density of the matrix and of
the vector is $1/2$. Density is pinned rather than swept because the fused
kernel is a straight-line vector loop whose instruction stream does not branch
on operand values (`crates/gf2-core/src/matrix.asm.txt`, symbol
`and_popcnt_fn`); the one data-dependent branch in the allocated boundary is
the output append, and at density $1/2$ the row parities it appends are
themselves near-balanced, which is the unfavourable case for a branch predictor
and the one this addendum pins. A sparse-matrix question belongs to `SpBitMatrix` and is
outside this addendum.

In `tail1` cells the final word of each row and of the vector is masked
immediately after generation so the padding bit is zero, and the same mask is
reapplied after every operation. The matrix and the vector are read-only in
every timed operation, so no cell needs a reset inside a timed window.

Both arms of a cell see the same fixture bytes. The M4RI arm receives those
same canonical gf2 words and writes them to public M4RI coordinates through
`mzd_write_bit`, reading results back through `mzd_read_bit`, exactly as the
matched-operation specification defines; no arm exposes M4RI storage as a gf2
word slice or compares physical row strides.

Before timed execution, every arm passes an untimed deterministic smoke over
logical column counts $0$, $1$, $63$, $64$ and $65$, all seven word counts,
full and `tail1` shapes, both cold and warm entry, and resume after each cell
boundary. The oracle is an independent row-parity computation in the harness, not a gf2
route; it checks every output bit, the output
length, canonical little-endian bit indexing, zero tail padding in the output
`BitVec`, and immutability of the matrix and the vector. The M4RI arm
additionally checks its fresh `mzd_t` allocations, the zeroing of non-window
excess bits, the packing and unpacking coordinates, and disposal. Smoke
execution emits no timing samples and cannot serve as a pilot.

## Cache, warmup, and sampling

All builds finish before timing and use release mode. Measurements run on the
prepared Ryzen 9 5900X under the repository's full-host benchmark-window lock.
The runner records the observed CPU IDs, topology, affinity, SMT, governors,
clocks, capabilities, toolchain, executable digests, and selected routes. The
gf2 baseline and candidate are `conservative-portable` Rust 1.95 release builds
with the workspace's ordinary all-feature configuration and no
`target-cpu=native` override; the scalar reference arm is the same
configuration with the `simd` feature removed. The M4RI arm is the separately
pinned `external` build of its qualification record. A tuned or native gf2
build is a different arm and requires a versioned amendment.

Cache policies are exact:

- `warm`: initialize the fixture, then execute exactly one untimed pass of the
  measured operation over that cell's complete working set before calibration.
  The pass uses the same buffers the timed call uses, including the output the
  timed call writes, and is observable from outside the process. A warm
  declaration whose pass omits the timed call's output buffer is a recorded
  defect that withdraws every comparison it supports
  (`dev/active/6fb89a3c/findings.md` § 5, *Warm-pass defect*), and an arm of
  this addendum that cannot demonstrate its pass records the cell unavailable.
- `streaming`: create eight fixture banks of at least 8 MiB each, rounded up to
  an integral number of complete matrix-and-vector tuples. Touch every
  initialized byte outside timing, do not execute the measured operation as
  warmup, and rotate banks once per operation. The reported working set is at
  least 64 MiB. Rotation is the claim; no cache-level eviction is claimed.
- `cold`: use a fresh child and fresh fixture, execute no measured operation
  before the first window, and fix `cold_calls` to one. It is a first-use
  series, not a hardware cache-miss-latency claim.

Every exploratory cell runs exactly 24 paired executions, the protocol's pilot
maximum, except the rows this addendum declares unavailable in advance, which
run none. Every confirmatory cell runs exactly 24 fresh paired executions. Each
pair launches adjacent fresh baseline and compared-arm children in the
protocol's seed-determined, two-pair-counterbalanced order. Each warm or
streaming execution uses five windows targeted at 100 ms after calibration; a
cold execution uses five one-call windows. No adaptive sample extension, early
significance stop, or reuse of exploratory pairs is allowed. The child timeout
is 120 seconds.

The outlier policy removes nothing. A window at least twice its execution
median is flagged; a flagged fraction above 0.10 makes the cell unstable and
non-qualifying. The attempt and search budgets remain spent.

## Estimator, confidence, and multiple comparisons

The resampling unit is one whole paired execution. For baseline medians $b_i$
and compared-arm medians $c_i$, the estimator is

$$
\hat{s}=\frac{\operatorname{median}_i b_i}
               {\operatorname{median}_i c_i},
$$

so $\hat{s}>1$ favors the compared arm. The interval is the protocol-v4
nearest-rank percentile bootstrap of 10,000 whole-pair resamples, using its
xoshiro256** stream seeded through SplitMix64. The family error rate is 0.05.
Attempt $t$ spends $\alpha_t=0.05/[t(t+1)]$ and Bonferroni uses
$\alpha_c=\alpha_t/m$, where the append-only ledger supplies $t$ and the
cumulative reserved comparison count $m$. A reservation counts a family's
non-exploratory cells only, at both the reservation and the verification site
(source evidence, claims on
`dev/tools/tuning-campaign-support/src/trial_ledger.rs`). This addendum
therefore gives the `exploratory` role to every row it declares unavailable in
advance, so those rows are retained in each receipt with their reason and zero
samples and spend no comparison. A cell that carries a confirmatory role spends
its comparison whatever its outcome, including an unavailability the runner
discovers at execution time.

Each canonical question owns a distinct ledger and family identity. The
version-1 prior-counter fields stay empty in every transcription; the ledger
chain alone controls the correction. Candidate selection families set
`holdout.required` to false with no holdout cells; final integration freezes its
own holdout family before observing after-change samples.

| Family identity | Canonical ledger | Confirmatory cells | Maximum $m$ in its first confirmation |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `2037941f-dense-isolated-fused-parity` | `dev/bench_results/2037941f/dense-isolated-fused-parity-ledger.jsonl` | five anchor isolated cells | 5 |
| `2037941f-dense-allocated-matvec` | `dev/bench_results/2037941f/dense-allocated-matvec-ledger.jsonl` | five anchor allocated cells | 5 |
| `2037941f-dense-matvec-vs-m4ri` | `dev/bench_results/2037941f/dense-matvec-vs-m4ri-ledger.jsonl` | two qualified comparator cells | 2 |

Each family's ledger is created once as an empty genesis file before that
family's first campaign and retained with every stage and receipt, so its first
confirmation is attempt $t=1$ and spends
$\alpha_1=0.05/(1\cdot 2)=0.025$. P-20 requires at least twenty expected
bootstrap draws in each tail, that is $10000\,\alpha_c/2\ge 20$, hence
$\alpha_c\ge 0.004$ and $m\le 0.025/0.004=6.25$, so a first confirmation
admits at most six comparisons. The three families stand at:

| Family | $m$ | $\alpha_c=0.025/m$ | Expected draws per tail $=10000\,\alpha_c/2$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| isolated fused parity | 5 | 0.005 | 25 |
| allocated whole-matvec | 5 | 0.005 | 25 |
| M4RI comparator | 2 | 0.0125 | 62.5 |

A second confirmation in any of these families is forbidden both by the
one-attempt-per-candidate rule and by that floor: attempt $t=2$ spends
$\alpha_2=0.05/(2\cdot 3)=0.008\overline{3}$, which supports at most
$0.008\overline{3}/0.004=2.08$, that is two cumulative comparisons, and each
family's first attempt already reserves two or five. Candidate pilots within
one question remain in that question's ledger and spend zero comparisons.
Candidate selection occurs only among those pilots; the selected identity
receives the family's single confirmatory reservation. Final integration uses a
separately frozen `final-integration` family because it asks the new
production-route question, not a renamed retry of candidate selection.

The decision interval $[\ell,u]$ follows protocol version 4: `improved` when
$\ell$ reaches the worthwhile or material-gap threshold, `not-worse` when
$\ell\ge 1/\theta_{\mathrm{eq}}$, `regressed` when $u<1/\theta_{\mathrm{eq}}$,
and `inconclusive` otherwise. Negative, not-material, unstable, inconclusive,
unavailable, and inapplicable rows stay in the receipt.

## Effect, resolution, and complexity rules

The pilot-derived resolution $r$ of a family is the largest relative bootstrap
half-width over its confirmatory cells at that family's corrected $\alpha_c$,
rounded upward to the next 0.001 and pinned with the pilot receipt and digest
in the confirmatory JSON addendum by the canonical freezer. A second
independent deterministic bootstrap must also fit $r$, as protocol version 4
requires. If $r$ exceeds the family ceiling below, the family records
`resolution-insufficient` and runs no confirmation. Margins are fixed here and
strictly exceed one plus the family's ceiling.

Each ceiling is derived from the closest measured analogue on this host, the
count and fused-reduction confirmation of `5cbb6545`, whose cells are the same
kernel and the same public `matvec`:

Each analogue half-width below is recomputed as $(u-\ell)/(2\hat{s})$ from the
*Speedup [interval]* column of the named row, rounded to four decimals; the row
is the authority and the arithmetic is shown so a reader reproduces it.

| This family | Analogue cells (`dev/bench_results/5cbb6545/tables.md` § Cells: `fused-confirmation`) | Analogue relative half-width at its $\alpha_c=0.0041\overline{6}$ | Ceiling declared here |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| isolated fused parity | `and-w128-fused`, `and-w512-fused`, `and-w4096-fused` | 0.0045, 0.0045, 0.0055 | 0.020 |
| allocated whole-matvec | `matvec-1024x4096`, `matvec-1024x16384` | 0.0216, 0.0267 | 0.035 |
| M4RI comparator | the same two allocated cells, widened for external construction and conversion | 0.0216, 0.0267 | 0.040 |

The analogue's corrected level, $0.0041\overline{6}$, is below the isolated and
allocated families' $0.005$ and far below the comparator family's $0.0125$, so
each analogue half-width is a conservative stand-in for the same cell measured
under this addendum. The ceilings leave a factor of 3.6 above the widest
isolated analogue and 1.3 above the widest allocated analogue; the comparator
ceiling is the widest because its arms add construction, packing and unpacking
loops whose per-call cost is comparable to the product itself.

| Family | Worthwhile speedup | Equivalence margin | Material gap | Resolution ceiling | One plus ceiling | Complexity budget and rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| isolated fused parity | 1.10 | 1.03 | none | 0.020 | 1.020 | One new unsafe kernel function with an explicit safety contract in `crates/gf2-kernels-simd/src/x86/`, 96 added nonblank production lines, no allocation, dependency, public API, semantic type, or second dispatcher. |

The M4RI comparator family declares no worthwhile speedup because it holds no
improvement or non-regression cell; its only objective is the comparator gap,
so its transcription leaves that one field unresolved by design rather than by
omission, and no cell of that family depends on it.

Every declared margin strictly exceeds one plus its ceiling: $1.10>1.020$ and
$1.03>1.020$; $1.08>1.035$ and $1.05>1.035$; $1.10>1.040$ and $1.05>1.040$.

The margins are tied to each other by the cost boundary. The allocated family's
worthwhile speedup is the smallest consumer gain this family can confirm at its
own resolution: the protocol rejects a margin at or below one plus the
resolution, so 1.035 is this family's floor and 1.08 is the first round value
above it that leaves the interval's lower bound room to reach the margin. The
isolated worthwhile speedup is then 1.10, because the fused kernel holds most
but not all of the allocated consumer at the attributed 64-word shape, so an
isolated gain no larger than the allocated bar cannot produce a confirmable
allocated gain. The equivalence margins say how much
slower a candidate may be at the family confidence: three percent on the
isolated kernel, where the analogue's resolution is small, and five percent on
the allocated consumer and the comparator, where it is not. A non-inferiority
statement finer than five percent is not available at this family's resolution,
and that limit is recorded rather than tightened.

The 96-line budget is twice the 42 nonblank lines of the established fused
kernel body plus twelve lines for the bundle field, its wrapper, the safety
comment, and the selector binding. The established body's extent is a pinned
source claim; a candidate larger than twice it is a different maintenance
question and needs its own container.

Adoption of a dense-parity candidate requires all of:

1. every allocated whole-matvec anchor is `improved` at the family's
   worthwhile speedup, so the benefit survives output allocation and the
   per-row entry;
2. every isolated anchor is `improved` or `not-material` and none is `fail`,
   `regressed`, or `unstable`, so the allocated result rests on a kernel that
   is nowhere slower and has a kernel attribution where it gains;
3. exact parity equivalence on the shared scalar and SIMD behavioural suites,
   including the 0, 1, 63, 64 and 65 boundary cases and zero tail padding;
4. the complexity budget above, with the scalar fallback retained.

An isolated improvement without condition 1 is an isolated result and selects
nothing. A comparator-gap outcome selects nothing in any case: an external
arm's lead is attributed, not adopted, and M4RI throughput cannot authorise an
incompatible production route. Where no candidate meets all four conditions the
established fused AND-popcount route is retained and every losing cell stays in
its receipt.

## Search and stopping rules

The frozen search budget is:

- at most one distinct fusion candidate identity, named by the downstream
  candidate portfolio from profile evidence;
- at most four exploratory pilot trials per cell across the pre-candidate
  identity baseline, the scalar reference arm, the candidate pilot, and one
  replacement for a pilot voided by a procedural defect in its own freeze or
  launch;
- exactly one confirmatory attempt for the selected identity and protocol
  version;
- no production change selected from isolated kernel throughput, from a
  comparator gap, or from a retained-state cell.

The pre-candidate campaigns use byte-identical public identity arms on both
sides, which retain the current absolute medians and the per-stride resolution
estimate and make no selection claim. Candidate campaigns replace only the compared build.

Profiles may eliminate candidates before a pilot and preserve the reason. They
may not introduce a second fusion identity, a threshold edit, a new output
contract, a caller-supplied output buffer, or an unlisted consumer. Search
stops when the budgeted identity has one completed pilot, when a profile rules
it out, or when the per-cell pilot cap is reached, whichever comes first. An
unstable or inconclusive confirmation is terminal for that identity and
protocol version. Sampling never expands to chase a margin.

## Preserved no-win and scope exclusions

The generic carry-save comparator is a preserved non-qualifying result and is
not a candidate here. Its confirmation records the fused carry-save route as
`inconclusive` against the established nibble lookup in the allocated
1024-row, 4096-column `matvec` and in the 128-word fused kernel, so the family
records `qualifies: false` and production retains the established routes over
the whole automatic-dispatch domain
([`dev/active/5cbb6545/findings.md`](../5cbb6545/findings.md) § Question and
answer, conclusions 1 and 5, and § What production takes, and the rule that
decided it; rows `matvec-1024x4096` and `and-w128-fused` of
[`dev/bench_results/5cbb6545/tables.md`](../../bench_results/5cbb6545/tables.md)
§ Cells: `fused-confirmation`). The same table records that at 4096 columns the
carry-save selector does not even choose its own carry-save lane, and that its
one improved allocated cell is at 16,384 columns, four times the story's
4096-column anchor. The carry-save crossover therefore lies above this addendum's band, and
the kernels stay in the bundle as direct comparators that no automatic resolver
selects (source evidence, claim on `crates/gf2-kernels-simd/src/lib.rs`).

This addendum accordingly forbids a candidate that re-runs the carry-save
comparator, that edits the selector threshold, or that relabels the already
selected fused AND-popcount route as new optimization work. It also excludes
the transpose and BCH-generator M4RI families, which are a different operation,
and the sparse `SpBitMatrix` parity route, which is a different consumer.

The predecessor bit-storage profile bounds this addendum in two ways. Its
confirmation records the generic dispatch hoist as not material on 64-word rows
([`dev/active/04b85d10/findings.md`](../04b85d10/findings.md) § Question and
outcome, item 3; row `logical-row-xor-dispatch-64w-1core` of
[`dev/bench_results/04b85d10/tables.md`](../../bench_results/04b85d10/tables.md)
§ `2026-09-10-04b85d10-logical-v3-confirmation`), and its attribution of the
allocated 1024-row, 4096-column `matvec` reads the dispatch probe below one
nanosecond per call (§ Attribution, row *Dense matvec 1024x4096*). The public
`matvec` resolves its route once per call, so no cell here proposes hoisting
that resolution out of the call. The bit-storage costs that same survey records
in consumers are planning evidence, not a current pre-change baseline; this
addendum's own pre-candidate campaigns supply that baseline.

A materially different future dense consumer needs a new versioned addendum; it
cannot reuse these samples or reopen either preserved result.

## Receipt and stopping evidence

Every campaign snapshots this document, the measurement contract, protocol,
schema, campaign JSON, producing-source closure, executable, toolchain, and
inputs by SHA-256 before opening its append-only log. The log path is printed
before the first bounded run, and completed cells resume without repetition; a
session that stops inside a cell is closed with an `interrupted` record and its
attempt abandoned once by the next session before that cell is measured again.
Receipts retain exact commands, raw pairs and windows, seeds, cache claims,
observed routes and operand alignment, fixture-bank sizes, output validation,
assembly, profiles, host facts, statistical decisions, and every negative,
unavailable, or inapplicable row.

A current pre-change baseline and a fresh after build are mandatory for any
production adoption. Navigation commits and unrelated worktree changes are
informational; only producing content identities decide validity. No finite
search is reported as an absolute optimum.

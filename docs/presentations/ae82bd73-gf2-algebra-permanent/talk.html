Background · 3 / 14

The permanent, and the bipedal trick

\[ \operatorname{perm}(A) = \sum_{\sigma \in S_n} \prod_{i=1}^{n} A_{i,\sigma(i)} \]

Ryser's formula evaluates it in O(n·2ⁿ) time via Gray-code subset sums. The "bipedal" map (arXiv 2407.20205) encodes F₃ⁿ as a pair of F₂ⁿ vectors — field add / sub / mul collapse to 1–6 bitwise u64 operations.

Capability 2 · 6 / 14

Parallel scaling

The permanent is embarrassingly parallel over independent Gray-code subranges. A Rayon work-stealing dispatch with a cache-tuned chunk size gives near-linear scaling.

Scaling factor ≥ 0.883× per physical core at n ≥ 28, measured across 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 12 threads on a 12-core Zen 3 host.

Capability 5 · 9 / 14

perm→uniform convergence, reproduced

A random permanent over a finite field converges to uniform far faster than the determinant (HKS Theorem 1.2 / arXiv 2407.20205). The GPU high-N re-sample resolves the convergence where CPU sampling could not:

18 cells — TVDperm ≤ TVDdet at 95% confidence at every cell. The q=3 permanent TVD collapses to ≈10⁻⁴ by n = 10, below the Monte-Carlo resolution floor even at 8M samples.
